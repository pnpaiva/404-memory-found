Tickle Me Elmo Worth: What a 1996 Original Sells For

By Theo Lindqvist · Published November 27, 2026 · 5 min read

A 1996 Tickle Me Elmo is worth about $30 to $40 loose and working, and roughly $75 to $100 in its original box, according to collector guides from 2024 and 2026. The doll listed for $28.99 and briefly resold for up to $1,500, but Tyco went on to make millions, so only clean boxed originals carry a premium today.

What does a 1996 Tickle Me Elmo sell for now?

A 1996 Tickle Me Elmo is worth tens of dollars, not thousands. A loose, working doll usually brings $30 to $40, and one still in its original box sits around $75 to $100, according to collector guides published in May 2024 and March 2026.

Those ranges are guide estimates, and asking prices run higher: Hello! noted in March 2026 that one TMX was listed for almost $250.

The gap between $1,500 in 1996 and $40 today comes down to one number: how many of these dolls Tyco made once the shortage ended.

Why are most Tickle Me Elmos worth so little?

Tyco Preschool released the doll in July 1996 with a first run of 400,000 units at a list price of $28.99, according to People's figure cited on Wikipedia. Mental Floss, in its 2017 oral history, put the shelf price at $29.95. Either way, the shortage was brief. Tyco ordered 600,000 more before the holidays and had sold about 1 million by the end of December.

Then the factories caught up. Former Tyco Preschool sales executive Tim Cleary told Mental Floss the company sold about 4 million more in 1997. A toy made by the millions cannot stay scarce, the arithmetic that also flattened Beanie Babies a few years later.

"The culture has moved beyond that, the whole hot-toy phenomenon," toy analyst Chris Byrne told USA Today in 2006. Today the money sits only in clean, boxed examples, which raises the practical question of how to tell a real 1996 Tyco doll from the many Elmos that followed it.

How to tell a 1996 Tyco original from later Elmos

Start with the tag. Original dolls carry the Tyco name: a museum example at The Henry Ford, dated 1997, has a sewn tag reading "TYCO" with a Jim Henson Productions copyright and a Tyco Preschool Toys copyright line, plus the item number 32715 on the back. Its box shows the Tyco and Sesame Street logos, a Duracell "BATTERY INCLUDED" panel and the words "TRY ME!" The doll runs on three AA batteries.

Anything without Tyco on the tag is a later line. The main versions collectors sort by:

  • 1996 and 1997, Tyco: the original Elmo, followed in early 1997 by Tickle Me Ernie, Big Bird and Cookie Monster.
  • Fall 2001, Surprise Edition: a contest version tied to a prize drawing held on January 9, 2002.
  • September 19, 2006, TMX: the tenth anniversary doll that rolls over and slaps the floor while laughing.
  • June 2026, Giggle Max: an 18-inch reissue from Just Play for the 30th anniversary, listed at $39.99.

A box that is torn, sun-faded or missing its cardboard insert pulls a doll back toward the loose price, and so does a battery compartment with corrosion.

Where to find one today: loose and boxed Tyco Elmos turn up all the time on Etsy's Tickle Me Elmo listings, where a working loose doll should cost about what the table above shows. Ask to see the tag before paying box prices. None of that explains why one of these sold for more than a used car in 1996, which is where the story really starts.

The $18,500 Elmo and the shortage nobody planned

The toy began as Tickles the Chimp, a laughing plush invented by Ron Dubren and Greg Hyman. "To be an inventor is an honor," Hyman once said, words The Strong National Museum of Play keeps on his profile; he died on May 1, 2026, at 78. Tyco first wanted a Tasmanian Devil version, then switched the idea to Elmo when its Looney Tunes license lapsed.

Rosie O'Donnell showed the doll on her talk show in October 1996. After Thanksgiving, Kay-Bee and Target sold out within an hour, the start of a run that emptied stores like KB Toys for weeks. "Plan a shortage? No one plans a shortage," former Tyco Preschool president Neil Friedman told Mental Floss in 2017.

On December 14, 1996, a crowd of about 300 shoppers at a Wal-Mart in Fredericton, New Brunswick, trampled clerk Robert Waller when he opened a box of Elmos. A doll reportedly sold for $7,100 in Denver, and on December 20 a Los Angeles pharmacy paid $18,500 for one at a KBIG radio charity auction. Two years later Furby took the crown, and the Elmo that once cost more than a used car settled into a $40 attic find.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is an original 1996 Tickle Me Elmo worth?

An original 1996 Tyco Tickle Me Elmo is usually worth $30 to $40 loose and working, and about $75 to $100 in its original box, according to collector guides from May 2024 and March 2026. Dolls that do not laugh or shake, or that have battery corrosion, sell for less.

How much did Tickle Me Elmo cost in 1996?

Tickle Me Elmo had a list price of $28.99 when Tyco Preschool released it in July 1996, although Mental Floss gives the shelf price as $29.95. During the December 1996 shortage, scalpers asked up to $1,500, and one doll sold for $18,500 at a Los Angeles charity auction.

Do they still make Tickle Me Elmo?

Yes. For the 30th anniversary, toy company Just Play released the 18-inch Tickle Me Elmo Giggle Max with a list price of $39.99, opening presales in June 2026 and reaching wider retail in mid-July. It is a new toy, so it has no bearing on what a 1996 Tyco original is worth.
