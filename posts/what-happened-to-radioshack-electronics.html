Is RadioShack Still in Business? The Stores Left by State

By Marcus Vale · Updated November 21, 2026 · First published March 30, 2026 · 10 min read

RadioShack is still in business in 2026 as a brand, a website and a network of independent dealers, not as a chain. Unicomer Group of El Salvador has owned the RadioShack name worldwide since May 2023, and no company-owned RadioShack stores are left in the United States. The company says more than 170 authorized dealers carry its products, and its own store locator listed 103 dealer entries in 38 states on November 21, 2026.

What RadioShack became after the chain died

The RadioShack that exists in 2026 is a product brand, not a store chain. Unicomer Group relaunched it in the United States in August 2024 as a wholesale and online business, and the red R now turns up on batteries, power adapters, memory cards and portable speakers sold in other people's shops, Modern Retail reported in May 2026.

About 60 percent of product sales came from business to business deals that spring, with the rest split between radioshack.com and online marketplaces. The catalog held about 600 products, with a target of about 1,000.

The surprise is what sells. Turntables and radios, often styled with wood cabinets or neon colors, made up about a third of the business. "Some of our most explosive sellers have been vintage items like turntables and radios," Luis Diego Cuestas, head of commercial at RadioShack USA, told Modern Retail in 2026.

Kevin Hamann, managing director of RadioShack USA, put the goal plainly to the trade magazine TWICE in October 2026. "We really want to be a destination for retro tech," he said. A destination needs a front door, though, and the front doors are what most people are searching for.

Where the last RadioShack stores are, state by state

No RadioShack store in the United States is owned by the company anymore. Every shop with the sign is an independent business signed up as an authorized dealer, usually an electronics, computer or repair store that also carries RadioShack products.

The company's own figure is more than 170 dealers in 40 states, the number Modern Retail reported in May 2026 and TWICE repeated in October. The radioshack.com homepage says more than 150. The official store locator, which Hamann told TWICE is the most visited page on the site, listed 103 dealer entries when this page checked it on November 21, 2026. A few shops appear twice, so the real count is about 100 shops in 38 states.

The map is overwhelmingly small town. None of the six Texas dealers is in Dallas or Fort Worth, where RadioShack kept its headquarters, and Alaska has as many as Alabama. Eleven listings still put RadioShack in the business name, such as Vern's RadioShack in Pocatello and Idaho Falls, or RadioShack of Sevierville in Tennessee.

Others simply added a RadioShack counter, among them two pharmacies, two auto parts stores, a True Value hardware store in Dowagiac, Michigan, and a furniture store in Kemmerer, Wyoming. A listing says nothing about how much stock is on the shelf, so call before driving. That patchwork is what is left of a network that once ran into the thousands.

Two ratios tell the whole story. In 1999 the company stores outnumbered the dealers more than two to one, and by June 2017 the dealers outnumbered them nearly seven to one. The company stores were the part that failed, and the reason starts with how they were built.

How a leather company put a RadioShack in every strip mall

Radio Shack opened in Boston in 1921, founded by the brothers Theodore and Milton Deutschmann to supply equipment for the new hobby of amateur radio. The name came from the small wooden structure that housed a ship's radio equipment.

Tandy Corporation, the Fort Worth leather goods company run by Charles Tandy, bought the struggling chain in the early 1960s for $300,000. It had nine stores and a mail order business. Tandy's formula was tiny stores, everywhere, stocked with the parts and accessories other retailers would not bother with.

It worked for more than three decades. Tandy Corporation's annual report counted 5,087 company-owned RadioShack stores in the United States on December 31, 1999, averaging about 2,300 square feet each, plus a separate network of 2,099 dealer and franchise stores.

The same counters sold computers. The TRS-80 arrived in 1977 and went out next to the speaker wire, which put RadioShack in the home computer race alongside Apple and Commodore. Tandy-era catalogs, Realistic audio gear and TRS-80 accessories still turn up on Etsy for collectors who want a piece of it.

The formula had one weak point. It depended on customers needing a part they could not find anywhere else, and that need was about to shrink.

Why cell phones sank the corner store

By the 2010s the parts business was fading, and RadioShack had bet its floor space on mobile phones and carrier commissions. The company, by then called RadioShack Corporation, lost $400.2 million in 2013 with 4,297 US stores, and on March 4, 2014 said it would close up to 1,100 of them.

It filed for Chapter 11 on February 5, 2015, listing $1.2 billion in assets against $1.39 billion in debts. The rescue sold stores to General Wireless, an affiliate of the hedge fund Standard General, with Sprint taking about a third of the space in 1,750 of them. "This agreement would allow Sprint to grow branded distribution quickly and cost-effectively in prime locations," Sprint CEO Marcelo Claure said in a statement reported by Fortune in 2015.

General Wireless kept about 1,740 stores open, and the arrangement lasted two years. It filed its own Chapter 11 on March 8, 2017, closing about 200 stores at once and putting the remaining 1,300 under review. After roughly 1,000 more closed starting Memorial Day weekend, Fortune counted 72 company-owned RadioShack stores left on June 1, 2017.

Circuit City and Fry's Electronics ran out of road in the same stretch of years. What set RadioShack apart is that its name kept getting sold, and the next buyer was the strangest one.

The crypto detour and the $112 million SEC lawsuit

In November 2020 the RadioShack brand went to Retail Ecommerce Ventures, a Miami firm run by the investors Alex Mehr and Tai Lopez. About 400 independently run RadioShack locations were still open at the time, the Associated Press reported. "We bought the raw material to build a big business," Mehr told the AP in 2020.

The business they built was crypto. By mid 2022 RadioShack was promoting itself as a crypto platform, and by late June 2022 the official RadioShack Twitter account was posting crude jokes to draw attention to it. When followers assumed the account had been hacked, it replied, "No we didnt get hacked, and no im not fired," as NBC News reported on June 30, 2022.

On September 25, 2025, the SEC sued Lopez, Mehr and a third executive, alleging that they raised about $112 million from hundreds of investors between April 2020 and November 2022, and that at least $5.9 million of the returns paid out were Ponzi-like payments funded by other investors. The SEC's announcement lists RadioShack Online LLC among the REV brands and alleges that the defendants "misappropriated approximately $16.1 million in investor funds for Lopez's and Mehr's personal use." Those were allegations, not findings, when the SEC announced the case.

The lawsuit targeted the three executives, not RadioShack's current owner. By the time it was filed, the brand had already passed to a company that had been running RadioShack stores for 25 years.

Why a retailer from El Salvador ended up owning RadioShack

Unicomer Group was a franchisee long before it was the owner. It took the RadioShack franchise in El Salvador in January 1998, and in 2015 it bought the brand rights and franchise agreements for Central America, South America and the Caribbean, according to the company.

On May 9, 2023, its affiliate Global Franchising Corporation bought RadioShack's intellectual property and domains in about 70 countries, including the United States, Canada and China. Unicomer put the brand's reach at more than 2,000 points of sale worldwide. "This acquisition will allow us to start another phase of growth and innovation in the shopping experience," Rudy Siman, Unicomer's vice president of franchises, said in the 2023 announcement.

Outside the US the company still runs hundreds of RadioShack stores, and its sourcing team works from El Salvador with an office in Hong Kong that walks the electronics fairs in Hong Kong and China, TWICE reported in October 2026. In the United States it chose not to rebuild the chain at all, which sends the story back to the oldest part of RadioShack.

Why the dealer stores outlived the chain

Dealer stores were never company assets. They were independently owned shops licensed to sell under the name, which is why 2,099 of them could exist beside the company stores in 1999 and why they came through both bankruptcies. Their number still shrank, from 943 at the end of 2013 to 500 in June 2017 and about 400 in 2020.

The company stores went fast in 2017, 200 at once and then about 1,000 more from Memorial Day weekend on, so leases lapsed in waves. That is where the familiar photos of abandoned RadioShacks come from, a 2,300 square foot box in a strip mall with the sign still up and a notice taped to the glass.

The dealers close one at a time. The last RadioShack in Maryland, in Prince Frederick, shut on April 26, 2025, after more than 40 years, a few months after its owner, Michael King, died at 79. With no US warehouse behind the name, the store had been buying its stock from other wholesalers. "It was fun while it lasted, but it's not the same anymore," his son Edward King told WTOP in 2025.

That dealer network is now the entire American plan. Hamann called the approach "capital-light" in TWICE, and the company is recruiting cellphone repair shops, independent computer stores and other local tech businesses. The chain that once ran 5,087 stores of its own now grows only when someone else's shop agrees to hang the sign.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many RadioShack stores are left in 2026?

RadioShack has no company-owned stores left in the United States in 2026; what remains are independently owned authorized dealers. RadioShack's official store locator listed 103 dealer entries in 38 states on November 21, 2026, led by Wisconsin with 7 and Texas with 6, while the company told Modern Retail and TWICE in 2026 that it works with more than 170. Outside the US, Unicomer Group still runs hundreds of RadioShack stores in Latin America and the Caribbean, according to TWICE in October 2026.

Who owns RadioShack now?

Unicomer Group, a retailer based in El Salvador, owns RadioShack through its affiliate Global Franchising Corporation, which bought the brand's intellectual property and domains in about 70 countries on May 9, 2023. Unicomer had run RadioShack stores as a franchisee since January 1998, starting in El Salvador. The previous owner, Retail Ecommerce Ventures, bought the brand in 2020, and in September 2025 the SEC sued its co-founders in a case brought against those executives, not against Unicomer.

When did RadioShack close?

RadioShack closed in stages rather than on a single day. RadioShack Corporation filed for Chapter 11 on February 5, 2015, its successor General Wireless filed again on March 8, 2017, and by June 1, 2017 about 1,000 more stores had closed, leaving 72 company-owned RadioShack stores, according to Fortune. Independent dealer stores have kept closing since, including the last one in Maryland, in Prince Frederick, on April 26, 2025.
