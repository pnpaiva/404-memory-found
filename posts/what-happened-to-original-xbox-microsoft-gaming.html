Used, boxed, sealed: the 2026 price spread

A tested original Xbox with a controller and cables sells for roughly $50 to $100 in 2026. PriceCharting's sold data for the console shows a tested unit at $50 and another at $76 on August 29, 2026, and that is the band most loose systems land in.

Boxed figures are where the price guides disagree, and the gap is wide enough to matter. BGR's 2026 look at the resale market, drawing on PriceCharting, puts a used console complete in box at an average near $274.85 and notes one that reached $750. SlashGear's 2026 guide says a complete console with a controller should sell for about $140 to $190 instead, and that recent sales sat near the low end.

Both are describing real listings. Boxed sales through the summer of 2026 ran from $131.50 to about $325, so the honest answer for a boxed system is a range rather than a figure.

Three ways to play the 2001 library in 2026

Microsoft's own route no longer needs the console at all. On November 12, 2026 the company added 21 original Xbox games to Xbox Backward Compatibility on PC, taking that list from four titles to 25, with The Elder Scrolls III: Morrowind and several Star Wars games among them. The games are sold individually, they are included in Xbox Game Pass plans, and a digital license you already own carries over.

That program started four months earlier. "Today, we're introducing Xbox Backward Compatibility on PC, making it easier to play classic Xbox games from the past across PC and handhelds, including the ROG Xbox Ally and Xbox Ally X," Xbox Wire wrote on July 22, 2026, launching with BLiNX: The Time Sweeper, Conker: Live and Reloaded, Crimson Skies: High Road to Revenge and Fuzion Frenzy. It requires Windows 11 and is limited to supported countries.

On consoles, the list is closed. Microsoft added its last batch of more than 70 backward compatible Xbox and Xbox 360 games on November 15, 2021, the console's 20th birthday, and has added none since. Published counts of how many 2001 titles are on that list differ: Tom's Guide puts it at 63 original Xbox games, while How-To Geek counted 39 before the 2021 batch landed.

The third route is the disc in your hand, which still boots. The obstacle is the television: the console outputs analog video, and most sets sold now have no component input, so it needs a converter such as EON's XBHD, listed at about $130 at Stone Age Gamer in November 2026. The fourth option, xemu, is free and open source, but it ships no firmware, so the BIOS has to come off a console you own.

None of that is Microsoft maintaining the 2001 machine, which is a separate question people keep asking.

Is the original Xbox still supported by Microsoft?

No. Microsoft ended production in 2006 and has given the 2001 hardware no first-party support since, and the Xbox Live service for it was switched off on April 15, 2010. Twenty years without a system update is the plain status.

The anniversary did not change that. The original Xbox turned 25 on November 15, 2026, and Microsoft marked the week with software and a lookalike: the 21 games added to Windows 11 on November 12, and the Xbox Series X25 Limited Edition, a translucent green Series X engraved with its own number and bundled with a download of Halo: Campaign Evolved, which went on sale on November 13, 2026 at $899.99 after pre-orders opened on August 26, 2026.

Nothing was announced for the console itself. That is a quiet ending for a machine Microsoft was willing to lose a fortune on.

How Microsoft built the Xbox and lost $4 billion on it

In 1998, four members of Microsoft's DirectX team, Seamus Blackley, Kevin Bachus, Ted Hase and Otto Berkes, started building a games machine out of PC parts after hours. Sony was about to ship the PlayStation 2, and was selling it as a living room computer rather than a toy.

The obstacle was internal. Microsoft in 1999 was a Windows company, and a rival team out of the WebTV group wanted a cheap set-top box running a cut-down copy of Windows. The DirectX plan called for custom silicon, a built-in hard drive and an Ethernet port, none of which looked like Windows at all.

So the engineers described it as a Windows device anyway. "We absolutely, intentionally hoodwinked [Bill Gates]," Blackley told GamesRadar in 2021, saying he had told Gates in writing, many times, that the team was building a Windows console. Microsoft had already tried to buy Nintendo and been turned down, so building the box was the only route left into the living room.

The Xbox reached US stores on November 15, 2001 at $299, a year after the PlayStation 2 and three days before the Nintendo GameCube. Inside the case was a 733 MHz Intel Pentium III, an Nvidia graphics chip, 64 MB of memory, a hard drive and an Ethernet port. No other console of that generation shipped with a hard drive and a network port as standard.

Those parts cost more than the sticker. Digital Trends' history of the console puts the build cost near $425 a unit against the $299 price, a loss of more than $100 before a single game changed hands. The plan was the usual console plan: give away the hardware and take a licensing fee on every game sold for it. That plan needs volume, and the volume never arrived. The Xbox sold about 24.65 million units worldwide before production ended in 2006, against more than 160 million for the PlayStation 2, a lifetime figure Sony finally confirmed in 2024. Microsoft's losses on the first Xbox ran past $4 billion.

What that money bought was not market share. It was two things nobody else had shipped.

What did Halo and Xbox Live actually change?

Microsoft bought Bungie in June 2000 and pulled Halo, then in development for Mac and PC, onto the Xbox as a launch title. Halo: Combat Evolved went on to sell about 6.4 million copies on the console, which works out to roughly one Xbox owner in four buying the same game.

Xbox Live followed on November 15, 2002, exactly one year after the console. Microsoft sold it as a $49.95 starter kit with a headset and a one-year subscription, and it was broadband only, with no dial-up option at a time when most American homes still had a modem. One account carried a single gamertag, one friends list and system-level voice chat across every game that supported it.

The payoff was Halo 2 on November 9, 2004. NBC News reported that week that the game sold 2.4 million copies and took $125 million in its first 24 hours, more than the opening day of any film to that point. Friends lists, party chat, matchmaking and downloadable map packs all became normal because they worked here first.

In one market, none of it helped.

The weekend Japan bought 123,929 Xboxes, then stopped

The Xbox launched in Japan on February 22, 2002 and sold 123,929 units over its opening weekend, a respectable start that fell apart within days. Buyers reported that the drive was scratching their discs, and some Japanese shops pulled the console off sale while Microsoft sorted it out.

Sales never recovered. Microsoft had shipped 190,092 units in Japan by the end of March 2002, and the console finished its life there under 500,000 units, a rounding error next to what Sony and Nintendo were doing at home.

The reasons stack up: a machine the size of a small suitcase, a controller Japanese players found unusable, and a library built around American shooters and sports games. Microsoft spent heavily on Japanese studios and still could not move the numbers. The Xbox brand has never recovered in that market.

The part of the Xbox that did come back, years later, came back without Microsoft.

Can you still play an original Xbox online in 2026?

Not on Microsoft's servers. Larry "Major Nelson" Hryb announced the end of the service on February 5, 2010, saying the change "will allow us to continue evolving the LIVE service with new features and experiences that fully harness the power of Xbox 360." Xbox Live for the original console went dark on April 15, 2010, taking Halo 2 matchmaking with it.

It came back as a volunteer project. Insignia, a free fan-built reverse engineering of the old Xbox Live servers, has been running in public beta since November 2022 and reopened Halo 2 matchmaking in March 2024 with ten playlists. Wikipedia's entry on Insignia put it at more than 36,000 registered users and 216 of the 381 Xbox Live games in November 2026, up from 199 games a year earlier, with matchmaking, leaderboards, friends lists, clans and voice chat working again.

The list is still growing. Pure Xbox reported in September 2025 that Need for Speed Underground 2 became the service's 200th game and its first Electronic Arts title, and in October 2026 that Burnout 3 and others had been added after a long pause. Insignia asks for no subscription and no modded console, only an original Xbox on broadband.

Which brings the question back to the hardware, and to the one component that decides whether a 25-year-old Xbox is worth buying at all.

What to check before you buy one

Ask the seller about the clock capacitor. On board revisions 1.0 through 1.5 the small capacitor that keeps the console's clock running leaks electrolyte onto the motherboard, and Hackaday's 2020 write-up of the problem called the result a corrosive death for the board. The usual fix is to remove the capacitor entirely and clean the board, after which the console runs fine and simply forgets the time when unplugged.

Revision 1.6 boards are the exception, as the consolemods wiki notes: their clock capacitor neither leaks nor comes out. If a listing shows photos of a clean board, or says the cap has been removed and the 3300 microfarad power supply capacitors replaced, that is worth paying for. Traces already eaten through need wire repairs and a soldering iron.

A working console with a controller and cables runs about $50 to $100 in 2026, and the parts that wear out cost real money on top: an S-type controller, a memory card, and the HD video gear the console needs on a modern television. Stone Age Gamer stocks original Xbox gear, including the XBHD adapter.

Twenty five years on, the machine that lost Microsoft $4 billion is the cheapest console of its generation to buy and the easiest one to keep running, which is not the ending anyone wrote for it in 2002.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is an original Xbox worth in 2026?

A tested original Xbox with a controller and cables sells for roughly $50 to $100 in 2026, and boxed examples sold between $131.50 and about $325 through the summer of that year. The averages quoted for complete in box systems disagree, from about $140 to $190 in SlashGear's 2026 guide to $274.85 in BGR's, so treat any single figure with suspicion. Graded consoles are a different market: only three have sold since 2023, at $1,650, $4,000 and $6,500.

How do you play original Xbox games on a PC?

Microsoft's Xbox Backward Compatibility on PC covered 25 original Xbox games as of November 12, 2026, including The Elder Scrolls III: Morrowind, bought individually or played through Xbox Game Pass on Windows 11. The alternative is xemu, a free open source emulator for the 2001 console, which does not supply the BIOS it needs, so that file has to be dumped from a console you own.

Does Xbox Live still work on the original Xbox?

Microsoft's Xbox Live service for the original Xbox was switched off on April 15, 2010, and it has not returned. Online play on the 2001 console now runs on Insignia, a free fan-run replacement that Wikipedia's entry recorded as supporting 216 of the 381 Xbox Live games with more than 36,000 registered users in November 2026, Halo 2 among them.
