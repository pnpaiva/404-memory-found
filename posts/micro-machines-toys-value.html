Are Micro Machines Worth Anything? How to Check Yours

By Theo Lindqvist · Published November 18, 2026 · 4 min read

Most Micro Machines are worth $2 to $15 for a single loose vehicle in good condition, and a boxed playset with its pieces sits in the low hundreds at most. The money is in unopened packaging: a sealed European double pack sold for $2,131 in December 2022, and recorded sales of the scarcest sealed collections average $185.85.

What sold prices look like, piece by piece

A single Micro Machine is worth a few dollars. A Micro Machines collection is worth whatever its best sealed piece brings, and nothing like the sum of its loose cars. The ranges below come from recorded completed sales and dated collector price guides, not from asking prices, which run far higher.

Two patterns run through those numbers. Loose plastic cars, the kind that lived in a carry case and under the couch, stay cheap however many you own. Sealed cards and factory-sealed collections hold nearly all the value, which is why one unopened set can outprice a bin of 200 loose vehicles.

Which versions do collectors actually pay for?

The line began in a toy store in Sturgeon Bay, Wisconsin, where Clemens V. Hedeen Jr. built two dozen miniature cars and their packaging by hand before taking them to a toy company. "The first and only company I sent my prototypes to was Galoob," Hedeen writes on his Fun City Toys page.

Lewis Galoob Toys turned that pitch into one of the biggest toy lines in the United States. By 1988, Micro Machines accounted for $60 million of Galoob's $140 million in revenue, and in 1989 the line reached $135 million of $228 million, according to the company history collected by Encyclopedia.com.

That scale is the reason most pieces are worth so little: Galoob made them by the tens of millions. What buyers chase is narrow.

  • Unopened cards and boxes. The top recorded sales are all sealed. The December 2022 record of $2,131 was a sealed European double pack, described in the listing as one of only six sealed sets known.
  • Named scarce collections. Sets such as Reflectors and the Emergency Collection carry the premiums in the sealed lists, with averages near $186 rather than near $20.
  • Paint and tooling variants. A master set offering 24 alternate paint colors and 8 variant versions recorded a $1,960 sale in June 2021.
  • First-wave Galoob packaging. Collector guides catalog the earliest vehicles as the 1987 Galoob Originals, marked with the Galoob name and a copyright year on the underside.

What knocks the price down?

Packaging does most of the work in both directions. Price guides note that sun-faded cards cut value sharply, and the same goes for a playset missing its ramps, stickers or cars. A boxed set that is 90 percent complete sells nearer the loose price than the sealed price.

Material matters too. Micro Machines were all plastic, so diecast collectors largely passed on them, and that has kept the floor low compared with Tiger Electronics handhelds or the 1990s card and disc crazes. Big mixed lots sell by the pound rather than by the piece, the same pattern that holds down Pogs and Beanie Babies today.

Where to find one today: loose vehicles and boxed playsets from the Galoob years turn up constantly on Etsy's Micro Machines listings, where single cars run a few dollars and complete boxed playsets usually sit in the low hundreds.

Are new Micro Machines still being made?

Hasbro owns the brand and licensed it to Jazwares, which put a new line of vehicles and playsets into Target and Walmart in August 2020. "Micro Machines is one of those collectible lines forever etched in pop culture and the minds of adults who adored them as kids," Jeremy Padawer of Jazwares told License Global in 2020.

That relaunch ran several waves. The collector site Joe's Curios logged Series 7 five-packs in US Target stores in July 2023 and recorded no new releases in 2024. New production is the quiet reason vintage prices have stayed flat: a shelf full of cheap new cars gives nobody a reason to bid up a loose 1989 casting.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is a big box of loose Micro Machines worth?

A box of loose Micro Machines is usually valued per car, not as a set, and recorded sales put common loose vehicles at $2 to $15 each in good condition as of November 2026. Large mixed lots sell at a discount to that, because buyers are hunting a handful of castings and absorbing the rest.

What is the most valuable Micro Machine ever sold?

The highest Micro Machines sale on record in collector price guides is a factory-sealed European double pack that brought $2,131 on December 28, 2022. A master set with 24 alternate paint colors and 8 variant versions sold for $1,960 in June 2021, and sealed sets from the scarcest collections have reached $668.88.

Are Micro Machines still sold in stores in 2026?

Micro Machines came back in August 2020, when Jazwares launched a new line under license from Hasbro at Target and Walmart. Collectors logged Series 7 five-packs in July 2023 and no new waves in 2024, so new stock in 2026 is whatever remains of that run rather than a fresh series.
