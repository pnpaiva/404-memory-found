Neo Geo AES Price: $649.99 New, What It's Worth Now

2026-10-14 | Updated 2026-11-04 | By Marcus Vale | 6 min read

The Neo Geo AES cost $649.99 in the United States as SNK's Gold System bundle, and single cartridges ran $200 to $300. SNK stopped making the home console in 1997 after about 410,000 sold worldwide. Price trackers now put a loose console between $567 and $1,020, and the reissue SNK and Plaion announced in 2026, the NEOGEO AES+, is due on September 16, 2027 at $249.99.

What did $649.99 actually buy at US retail?

SNK sold the Advanced Entertainment System, the AES, in two bundles. The Gold System listed at $649.99 with two arcade-style joysticks and a pack-in game. A cheaper Silver System came with one joystick and no game at $399.99.

SNK had planned a $599 US debut before the launch price went up. The dates are messier than the price: Wikipedia puts the North American AES release at July 1, 1991 and flags that date as unsourced, while other write-ups place the US arrival in 1990, the year SNK's MVS arcade boards reached American operators.

The hardware explains the number. The AES was not a scaled-down home machine running arcade ports. It used the same Motorola 68000 and Zilog Z80 arrangement as the MVS arcade system, which made a Neo Geo cartridge close to an arcade ROM board in a plastic shell.

Wikipedia's Neo Geo article calls it "the most expensive home video game console ever released at the time" once inflation is accounted for. SNK was not the last company to bet on a premium console either, as the 3DO's launch price would show a few years later.

Why did Neo Geo cartridges cost as much as a console?

Individual Neo Geo cartridges sold for $200 to $300 each. That was not a markup for prestige. Because SNK did almost nothing to adapt the arcade hardware for living rooms, a home cartridge carried something close to the full arcade ROM set, and ROM chips were the expensive part of any 1990s game.

The result was a console where one fighting game could cost more than a rival console with a game included. A buyer who wanted four Neo Geo titles was looking at roughly $800 to $1,200 in software on top of the $649.99 machine.

SNK's own answer arrived in 1994 with the Neo Geo CD, which moved the same library onto discs that cost a fraction of a cartridge and traded that saving for long load times. It never displaced the cartridge system among people who already owned one.

Those are 1990s dollars, and they do not translate gently. Our run through 90s tech prices in 2026 dollars covers how far the gap has widened on everything from consoles to camcorders.

How many Neo Geo AES consoles did SNK actually sell?

About 410,000 AES units worldwide, by the figure Wikipedia's Neo Geo article carries, against about one million MVS arcade units. Other pages put the AES and the Neo Geo CD together at 980,000 units as of March 1997, so the totals move depending on what is being counted, and the home side of the Neo Geo was always the smaller business.

The console almost did not reach shops at all. In Japan the AES began as a rental machine, the Neo Geo Rental System, supplied to game stores and commercial venues because manufacturing cost ruled out a normal retail price. SNK reversed that after demand held up and sold it as a premium product instead.

That shape held for the rest of its life. SNK stopped manufacturing the AES in 1997, seven years after the Japanese launch, while the MVS arcade platform carried on and software support ran to 2004. The company itself filed for bankruptcy in Osaka in October 2001, and the brand passed through Playmore and then SNK Playmore before returning to the SNK name in 2016.

Is the Neo Geo AES still worth money in 2026?

Yes, more than it cost new in nominal dollars, but the trackers disagree on how much. PriceCharting's Neo Geo AES console page lists the system at about $567 loose, about $1,128 with the box, about $1,700 complete and about $3,400 new, and notes that its most recent recorded sale is from May 2024.

SportsCardPro, which runs on the same PriceCharting sales data, shows the identical product at about $1,020 loose, about $2,912 complete and about $4,318 new. That is a gap of roughly 80 percent on a loose console between two guides reading the same market, so treat any single number as a starting point rather than a valuation.

Software is where the real money sits. PriceCharting puts the original Metal Slug around $3,225 loose and SportsCardPro around $3,358, and a handful of low-print titles reach five figures. A complete Neo Geo library is not a collection a normal person assembles.

Condition drives most of the spread. The AES saves to a memory card and ships with heavy joysticks that take abuse, so a tested console with a working card slot and one good stick is worth paying up for over an untested bargain.

Where to find one today. Budget around $600 to $1,000 for a tested loose console with one joystick, and considerably more if you want the box. A retro hardware specialist is the safer route for a 35-year-old system than an unknown seller, and Stone Age Gamer's catalog search is a reasonable place to start on consoles, memory cards and sticks.

Who makes the new Neo Geo console, and what does it cost?

SNK and the publisher Plaion announced the NEOGEO AES+ in April 2026 and opened pre-orders on April 16, 2026. The standard console lists at $249.99, a white Anniversary Edition at $349.99 and an Ultimate Edition at $999.99, with new cartridges around $90 each. SNK still owns the brand; Plaion's hardware arm, PLAION REPLAI, builds the machine.

The pitch is that it is not an emulation box. Plaion builds it around application-specific integrated circuits that reproduce the behavior of the original chips, and it accepts original AES cartridges rather than offering a built-in ROM menu. HDMI output and high score saving are the concessions to 2026.

Ben Jones, commercial director of PLAION REPLAI, said in the April 2026 announcement that "The NEOGEO remains peerless in the eyes of arcade enthusiasts".

Ten games were lined up to launch with the hardware, including Metal Slug and The King of Fighters 2002. The catch is the calendar.

Why has the AES+ slipped to September 2027?

Memory chips. The AES+ was set for November 12, 2026, and in September 2026 PLAION REPLAI moved the worldwide launch to September 16, 2027, about ten months later.

The company pointed at global component supply constraints, driven by memory demand from AI infrastructure spending, and said it could not manufacture enough systems for a worldwide launch. Its statement added: "We know this is a significant delay, and we're genuinely sorry."

Plaion said existing pre-orders keep their place in the production queue, that prices are unchanged across all three editions, and that consoles from the launch run carry a Founding Supporter Bonus. Nobody has to re-order.

For anyone weighing an original against the reissue, that is the live question in 2026. A loose original runs two to four times the price of a standard AES+ and is available now; the AES+ costs $249.99 and is a year away. The 1990 machine has held its value through worse news than a chip shortage, and the arcade hardware argument that made it expensive is the same one SNK is selling again. Readers who followed how the Sega Saturn landed in the collector market will recognize the pattern: scarce hardware plus an expensive software library equals a floor under the price.

Is a Neo Geo AES worth anything in 2026?

Yes. PriceCharting lists the Neo Geo AES console at about $567 loose, about $1,700 complete in box and about $3,400 sealed, while its SportsCardPro mirror puts the same console at about $1,020 loose and about $2,912 complete. Games carry the bigger premium, with the original Metal Slug above $3,000 loose and rare titles reaching five figures.

Is SNK making a new Neo Geo console?

Yes. SNK and Plaion announced the NEOGEO AES+ in April 2026, a hardware-based reissue that takes original Neo Geo AES cartridges, priced from $249.99. Its worldwide release moved from November 12, 2026 to September 16, 2027 after a memory chip shortage, and pre-orders placed in 2026 remain valid.
