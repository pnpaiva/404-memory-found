Are Nintendo Power Magazines Worth Anything Now?

By Marcus Vale · Published November 14, 2026 · 4 min read

Most Nintendo Power magazines are worth $10 to $30 each, and mixed lots of 20 to 40 issues sell for $60 to $150. The money is all in the July and August 1988 first issue: an ungraded copy with its poster intact brought $75 to $140 in late 2025, while a CGC 9.8 copy sold for $108,000 through Heritage Auctions in April 2023.

What recent sold prices look like

Almost every issue of Nintendo Power is a common magazine. Nintendo of America printed 3.6 million copies of the first issue alone and mailed them free to Nintendo Fun Club members, so supply is enormous and most copies change hands for pocket money. The money sits in two places: the very first issue, and copies that a grading company has sealed in plastic.

Every figure above is a range, not a quote on your copy. Grading, the poster, and whether a buyer wants that exact cover move the number more than the issue number does.

Which issues collectors actually pay for

Issue #1, dated July and August 1988, is the one with a market. It carried the Super Mario Bros. 2 cover and the maps for the second quest in The Legend of Zelda, and it is the only issue that has set auction records. A CGC 9.8 copy, the single highest-graded example in the CGC census at the time, sold through Heritage Auctions in April 2023. CGC said the copy "realized a record-shattering price of $108,000" and called it the most expensive Nintendo magazine ever sold. The previous high was a CGC 9.4 copy at $24,000 in November 2022.

Those numbers describe plastic-sealed, near-perfect copies that almost nobody has. Grade is the whole story. GoCollect's 2023 market report on the title made the floor clear: "Even at a 1.8, Nintendo Power #1 still sells for $150." The scarcer prize is the predecessor, Nintendo Fun Club News, which ran seven issues in 1987 and 1988 before Nintendo folded it into the magazine. A 4.0 copy of its first issue sold for $700 in 2021 and a 7.5 copy for $2,750, which is more than most graded Nintendo Power issues bring.

What knocks a copy's price down

Nintendo bound a pull-out poster, a Player's Poll card and a business reply card into early issues, and children pulled all three out. A copy missing the poster sells for roughly half what the same issue brings with it attached, judging by tracked sales of issue #1. Subscriber copies carry a mailing label printed straight onto the cover, which costs points in grading, and the same house that owned a stack for 30 years usually stored it in a basement, so rippled pages and a cracked spine are normal.

Volume matters too. The run reached 285 issues before Nintendo and publisher Future US ended it in December 2012, and sellers who list a full stack one issue at a time usually give up and sell it as a lot for a few dollars a copy. The same collapse hit other game magazines: Game Informer ran to 2024 and has no collector market at all outside its earliest issues.

Should you get a copy graded?

Only for issue #1 or a Fun Club News issue, and only if the copy looks sharp. Grading costs money per magazine and adds nothing to a 1994 issue that sells for $12 either way. The math works when a near-mint early copy can jump from under $150 raw to four figures sealed, which is the same pattern that reshaped cartridge prices for the Nintendo 64 and its games.

Where to find one today. Single issues and full runs turn up constantly from sellers who list magazines and paper ephemera, and most want $10 to $25 for a clean 1990s issue. Browsing the Nintendo Power listings on Etsy is the simplest way to compare covers and condition before paying grading-era prices for a copy you only want to read.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are Nintendo Power magazines worth anything without the poster?

Yes, but less. Tracked sales of the July and August 1988 first issue put copies with the poster intact at $75 to $140 in late 2025 and copies with the poster gone or partially torn out at $28 to $80. For issues from the 1990s and 2000s, a missing poster usually drops a $20 magazine to $10 or into a bulk lot.

What is the most valuable Nintendo Power issue ever sold?

A CGC 9.8 copy of Nintendo Power issue #1 from July and August 1988 sold for $108,000 through Heritage Auctions in April 2023, on a final bid of $90,000 plus a 20 percent buyer's premium. CGC, which graded it, called it the most expensive Nintendo magazine ever sold, and the earlier record was a CGC 9.4 copy at $24,000 in November 2022.

Can you read old Nintendo Power issues for free?

Yes. Scans of Nintendo Power, from the 1988 first issue to the December 2012 finale, along with the 1987 Nintendo Fun Club News run, are hosted free on the Internet Archive. Reading them there costs nothing, which is part of why common issues stay cheap on the second-hand market.
