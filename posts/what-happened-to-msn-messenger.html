Is MSN Messenger Still Around? Yes, on Escargot

By Theo Lindqvist · Updated November 8, 2026 · First published March 23, 2026 · 6 min read

MSN Messenger no longer exists as a Microsoft product. Microsoft announced the retirement on November 6, 2012, migrated users to Skype from April 8, 2013, and then retired Skype itself on May 5, 2025 in favor of Microsoft Teams Free. The original Messenger clients still work in 2026, but only against Escargot, a free fan-run server that rebuilt the protocol Microsoft switched off and that still had users signed in on November 8, 2026.

How Microsoft got into a market AOL already owned

MSN Messenger Service went live on July 22, 1999, about two years after AOL Instant Messenger and nearly three years after ICQ. Microsoft announced it the day before and priced it at nothing: the client was a free download, and it signed you in with the Hotmail address you already had.

"Communications continues to be the cornerstone of the Internet, and instant messaging is becoming a more prevalent way for people to communicate," said Brad Chase, then a Microsoft vice president, in the company's launch announcement in July 1999.

The shortcut Microsoft chose was interoperability. Version 1.0 let you message AOL Instant Messenger contacts from inside MSN Messenger, using AOL's servers, without an AOL account. AOL read that as theft of its network, changed its protocols and cut Microsoft off. Microsoft found a way back in. AOL blocked it again. The two traded workarounds for weeks before Microsoft dropped the feature, which never returned in any later version.

What AOL could not block was Windows. MSN Messenger did not have to be discovered, downloaded or recommended. It was already on the machine, in a decade when almost every machine ran Windows, and that carried it into countries where AOL had no presence at all.

What made MSN Messenger feel different from email

The software rewarded fiddling, which is why people remember it as a place rather than a tool. The display name was a public text field you could rewrite ten times in an afternoon, and people filled it with song lyrics, private jokes and messages aimed at exactly one person who would know.

Presence was the other half. "Appear Offline" made being unreachable a deliberate act that took one click and needed no explanation, and signing out at night was normal rather than a statement. There were no read receipts to argue about.

The feature everyone quotes arrived later than memory suggests. Nudges and winks shipped with MSN Messenger 7.0 in April 2005, alongside Xbox Live integration and handwriting input you scrawled with a mouse. A nudge shook the recipient's whole chat window and buzzed, and it was used almost exclusively to annoy people.

Messenger also sat in the middle of Microsoft's consumer stack. It raised Hotmail alerts, tied into the MSN Spaces blogging service and ran small games such as Minesweeper Flags inside the chat window. For a few years Microsoft had a working social network and treated it as a utility.

Why did Microsoft shut MSN Messenger down?

The name went first. In June 2006, with version 8.0, MSN Messenger became Windows Live Messenger, filing one of the best known brands on the internet under a corporate umbrella that meant nothing to the people using it. MSN Messenger 7.5 was the last release to carry the original name.

Usage kept climbing regardless. Microsoft said in June 2009 that more than 330 million people used the service every month, the highest figure the company ever gave for it.

Then the ground shifted twice. Facebook added chat in 2008, where the contact list was already built and the switching cost was zero. WhatsApp arrived in 2009 on phones. Messenger was built for a desk you sat down at and a session you consciously started, and that ritual was disappearing.

Microsoft had bought Skype in 2011 and chose to consolidate on it. Tony Bates, then president of Microsoft's Skype division, wrote on November 6, 2012: "We will retire Messenger in all countries worldwide in the first quarter of 2013 (with the exception of mainland China where Messenger will continue to be available)." Messenger still had more than 100 million users. Skype had 280 million, and Skype was the product Microsoft had just paid for.

The migration ran language by language from April 8, 2013. Mainland China, where the service was operated through a local partnership, kept Messenger until October 31, 2014.

Who runs MSN Messenger now that Microsoft does not?

Nobody at Microsoft. The server that answers when an old client signs in belongs to Escargot, an independent volunteer project that reimplemented the MSN Messenger and Windows Live Messenger protocols so the original software has something to talk to. Microsoft still owns the MSN name, now attached to msn.com, a news portal with no messaging product on it, and has announced no revival of its own.

Escargot costs nothing. An account needs an email address and a username, and the project lists MSN Messenger versions 1 through 7 and Windows Live Messenger 8 through 2009 as working, with the 2011 and 2012 releases still unsupported. Getting a client to connect usually means applying the project's Escargot Switcher patch first, because the old software has Microsoft's dead server addresses compiled into it.

It is still running in November 2026. Escargot's public stats page logs sign-ins by client version hour by hour, and one hourly snapshot on November 8, 2026 recorded 105 users on the Windows Live Messenger 2009 build alone. Quiet hours drop to single figures, so this is a small room rather than a comeback.

What no server can restore is the contact list. Everyone you want to talk to has to sign up as well, which is the problem Messenger solved in 1999 and the reason most people sign in once, look around and leave.

What replaced MSN Messenger, and what replaced that?

Officially Skype replaced it. In practice most Messenger users never arrived. They went to Facebook Messenger, WhatsApp or iMessage, because that is where their friends already were, and Microsoft inherited a shrinking share of a market it had once led.

The replacement did not outlast the thing it replaced by much. Skype shut down on May 5, 2025. "As of May 5, 2025, Skype is retired," Microsoft's support page states, adding that the change "affects both free and paid Skype users." Skype credentials now sign people into Microsoft Teams Free, which carried contacts, chats and call history across for anyone who moved.

The door closed behind them in January 2026. Microsoft said Skype chat logs and call history would stay reachable inside Skype only until then, so anyone who never signed in to Teams and never exported has no official copy left to collect.

So the line runs MSN Messenger to Windows Live Messenger to Skype to Microsoft Teams, and the only survivor of the four is a work tool with a calendar in it. The 2005 version, with the buzzing windows and the song-lyric display names, exists in exactly one place, and Microsoft does not run it.

Frequently Asked Questions

When did MSN Messenger shut down?

MSN Messenger, renamed Windows Live Messenger in June 2006, was retired language by language from April 8, 2013, after Microsoft announced the decision on November 6, 2012. Mainland China was the single exception and kept the service until October 31, 2014. Everyone was pushed to Skype, which Microsoft then shut down itself on May 5, 2025.

Can you still use MSN Messenger in 2026?

Yes, through Escargot, a free fan-run server that rebuilt the protocol Microsoft switched off in 2013. Install an old MSN Messenger or Windows Live Messenger client, apply the Escargot Switcher patch and sign in with an Escargot account. The project supports MSN Messenger 1 through 7 and Windows Live Messenger 8 through 2009, and it still logged users signed in on November 8, 2026. Microsoft offers no official way to run MSN Messenger.

Why did Microsoft replace MSN Messenger with Skype?

Microsoft bought Skype in 2011 and decided to run one consumer messaging product instead of two. When the retirement was announced on November 6, 2012, MSN Messenger had more than 100 million users against Skype's 280 million, so Skype won the merge. Most Messenger users went to Facebook Messenger or WhatsApp rather than Skype, and Microsoft retired Skype in turn on May 5, 2025.
