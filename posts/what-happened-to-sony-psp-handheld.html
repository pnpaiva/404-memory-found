Is the PSP Still Supported? Sony's Handheld in 2026

2026-03-31 | Updated 2026-10-19 | By Marcus Vale | 6 min read

The Sony PSP is not supported in 2026. Sony stopped making it, closed the handheld's own store in July 2021, and now sells PSP downloads only through the PS3 and PS Vita stores, which Sony confirmed in July 2026 will close in stages ending July 2027. Sony's last published sales figure for the PSP is more than 76.4 million units, against 154.02 million for the Nintendo DS.

How many PSPs did Sony actually sell?

Sony's corporate sales page still lists the PSP at “More than 76.4 million (As of March 31, 2012)” and has never replaced that line with a final lifetime total. Software on the platform stopped at more than 331.0 million units on the same date.

Sony kept shipping PSPs after 2012 and simply stopped updating the page, so higher numbers circulate without company backing. The 76.4 million figure is the one that traces back to Sony itself, which is why it is the number worth quoting.

The scale problem shows up when you put the handheld next to Sony's own hit. The same page credits the PlayStation 2 with more than 160 million units. The PSP, Sony's most successful portable by a wide margin, did not reach half of that.

None of that made the PSP a failure. It outsold every handheld anyone had launched against Nintendo up to that point, including Sega's Game Gear. It just never got close to the machine it was built to beat.

Why did the Nintendo DS win the handheld war?

Nintendo's investor relations site puts lifetime Nintendo DS hardware sales at 154.02 million units as of December 31, 2016, the point at which Nintendo stopped updating the line. That is roughly twice Sony's published PSP figure, and it still makes the Nintendo DS the best-selling handheld ever built.

Price did most of the damage. Sony Computer Entertainment America announced the PSP Value Pack at $249.99 in the United States, on sale March 24, 2005. The Nintendo DS had launched the previous November at $149.99. Sony was asking a hundred dollars more for a machine that arrived months later.

Sony was also not chasing the same buyer. Its launch pitch sold the PSP as portable entertainment rather than a games console: the Value Pack shipped with headphones, a 32 MB Memory Stick Duo and a soft case, and the first one million units came with a UMD copy of Spider-Man 2 from Sony Pictures.

Nintendo was selling something people already understood. Two screens, a stylus, cheap batteries, and a software library aimed at anyone who had ever owned a Game Boy. Sony was selling portable media in the same year the iPod became the default answer for portable media.

What was UMD, and why did PSP movies fade?

UMD stood for Universal Media Disc, a proprietary 1.8 GB optical disc Sony designed specifically for the PSP. Games shipped on it, and so did films. Sony's own launch bundle put a Spider-Man 2 UMD in the first million American Value Packs, which tells you how central the movie plan was.

Studios tried the format, then drifted away, and the discs became a novelty section that retailers quietly shrank. The clearest verdict came from Sony itself: when the PSP Go arrived, it had no UMD drive at all, and everything was downloaded to internal flash storage.

A hardware company does not remove its own format from its own product unless the format has stopped earning its place.

UMD movies also had a structural problem. A film on UMD played on exactly one device, at 4.3 inches, and could not be ripped, lent, or watched on a television without extra hardware. A DVD cost less and played anywhere.

Which PSP models did Sony release?

There were five. The PSP-1000 is the original, the heavy one, with the 4.3-inch 16:9 screen Sony built the whole pitch around. The PSP-2000, sold as Slim and Lite, cut weight and thickness and added video output. The PSP-3000 improved the screen and put a microphone in the chassis.

Then Sony got strange. The PSP Go dropped the UMD drive, shrank the body, hid the controls behind a sliding screen, and sold only downloads. It is the rarest of the five today and the most expensive second-hand.

The PSP-E1000 came last, a stripped-down budget model sold in Europe with no Wi-Fi and a single speaker. American buyers mostly never saw it, which is part of why the PSP lineup gets remembered as four machines rather than five.

All five play UMD games except the PSP Go, and all five are long out of production. Sony has not manufactured a PSP in over a decade.

Can you still buy PSP games in 2026?

Yes, for a little longer, and not on the handheld. Sony gives the PSP no support, no firmware work and no official repair path, so everything left runs through two other consoles on a clock.

The PlayStation Store on the PSP closed in 2016, and the handheld's remaining store searches and in-game purchases ended on July 6, 2021. PSP titles stayed on sale after that through the PS3 and PS Vita stores, which is where the format has been bought ever since.

On July 1, 2026 Sony confirmed that those two stores are closing as well, in stages. The PlayStation Store on PS3 shuts down in Mexico, Honduras and Nicaragua starting August 2026, in further Latin American and Middle Eastern countries in late 2026, and in every other country, on both PS3 and PS Vita, in July 2027.

Sony blamed the plumbing rather than the audience. “PS3 and PS Vita are no longer able to support these updates at the level required,” Sid Shuman of Sony Interactive Entertainment wrote in the July 2026 announcement, pointing at modern payment processing standards. Content already bought stays downloadable after each closure.

The PS Vita, Sony's follow-up handheld, never got a proper obituary either. Sony's sales page carries a single footnote on the subject: “Sales data on PlayStation Vita are not disclosed.” It is the only PlayStation system the company declines to put a number on.

What keeps the PSP running in 2026 is the homebrew scene. Custom firmware turns the handheld into an emulator box, a media player and a way to load game backups from a memory card, and hobbyists have maintained it for longer than Sony supported the machine officially.

What does a used PSP cost today?

PriceCharting's tracked sales put a loose PSP-1000 in black at $79.99 and a complete boxed one at $153.07. The later models cost more, and the PSP Go is the one that stings: it trades above $140 loose, more than the machine cost new at several points in its life.

Condition drives most of that spread. The analog nub wears out, the battery swells and the UMD door snaps, so a cheap listing is often a repair project. Limited editions run higher again, and the games are the affordable part of the hobby.

Where to find one today: tested PSP systems and refurbished shells generally trade between $80 and $200 depending on model. Sellers who service the batteries and nubs before shipping list on Etsy's Sony PSP console listings.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many PSPs were sold compared with the Nintendo DS?

Sony's last published figure for the PSP is more than 76.4 million units as of March 31, 2012, and the company never issued a final lifetime total. Nintendo reports 154.02 million Nintendo DS units as of December 31, 2016, so the Nintendo DS outsold Sony's handheld by roughly two to one.

When did the PSP store shut down?

Sony closed the PlayStation Store on the PSP in 2016 and ended the handheld's remaining store searches and in-game purchases on July 6, 2021. PSP titles stayed on sale through the PS3 and PS Vita stores, which Sony confirmed in July 2026 will close in stages running from August 2026 to July 2027.

What PSP models are there?

Sony released five PSP models between 2004 and the end of production: the original PSP-1000, the slimmer PSP-2000, the PSP-3000 with its improved screen, the download-only PSP Go with no UMD drive, and the budget PSP-E1000 sold in Europe. All except the PSP Go take UMD discs.
