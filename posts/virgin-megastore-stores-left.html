Who Owns Virgin Megastore Now? Zero US Stores Left

2026-09-25 | By Dana Reyes | 6 min read

Virgin Megastore closed its last six US stores in the summer of 2009, thirteen years after the Times Square shop opened at 1540 Broadway. The name survived the stores: Virgin Group still licenses it, and Azadea Group runs more than 40 Virgin Megastores across nine markets in the Middle East and North Africa. There has been no US location since 2009.

How a British record chain got a foothold on Sunset Boulevard

Virgin Megastore reached the United States in December 1992, when the first American store opened on Sunset Boulevard in Hollywood.

The format came straight from London. One enormous room, hundreds of thousands of titles, listening posts, and clerks who would argue about a record instead of just scanning it.

US music retail in 1992 was already crowded. Tower Records held the West Coast, mall chains such as Sam Goody held everywhere else, and discount stores were using CDs as loss leaders to pull shoppers through the door.

Virgin's answer was size and spectacle. A megastore was not meant to be the cheapest place to buy an album. It was meant to be the place a visitor walked into because the sign was enormous and the building was open until midnight.

That worked for a decade. Virgin Megastore North America grew to 23 stores by 2002, and those stores brought in about $230 million a year, The Hollywood Reporter reported in 2009. It was the last national music chain of its size to open in America rather than shrink in it.

What made the Times Square store the biggest record shop in America

The store that defined the chain opened at 1540 Broadway on Times Square in 1996 and stayed there until 2009.

It was not a normal shop. CoStar reported in 2008 that Virgin leased 65,337 square feet at 1540 Broadway, plus two floors at 4 Union Square a few miles downtown. Both buildings belonged to Vornado Realty Trust.

Richard Branson worked the opening the way he worked everything else. On his Virgin.com blog he wrote that Foo Fighters and Fugees played and that he was lowered from a height to cut the ribbon, and he called the result the most successful record store in America.

The claim was not just promotion. For most of its run the Times Square store carried the chain, because it sat in the middle of the densest tourist corridor in the country and stayed open past midnight.

It was also a physical argument in a fight the industry was losing. The store opened three years before Napster and five years before the iTunes Store, and by 2007 a shopper who wanted one song no longer had to walk into a building at all.

Why two real estate firms bought a profitable record chain

In 2007 the US arm of the business, Virgin Entertainment Group North America, was bought by Related Companies and Vornado Realty Trust. Neither company was in the music business.

The reason was rent. Virgin had signed long leases in the 1990s, before Times Square became one of the most expensive retail addresses in the world, and it was still paying 1990s money.

Sandeep Mathrani, Vornado's executive vice president for retail real estate, was blunt about it in remarks to Reuters in 2008 that CoStar reported: "We bought the Virgin business to wind it down to get a hold of the real estate."

Mathrani put Virgin's Times Square rent at $54 a square foot against a market rate of roughly $700 a square foot for that stretch of Broadway. At 65,337 square feet, that gap was worth more every year than the store could earn selling compact discs.

This is the part people usually get wrong about Virgin Megastore. The stores were not bankrupt and were not losing money when they closed. They were sitting on land that was worth more without them.

When did the last US Virgin Megastore close?

The wind-down was announced in March 2009 and finished that summer.

The Times Square flagship closed in mid-April 2009. A second New York store and the outlets in Los Angeles, San Francisco, Orlando and Denver followed by June. About 1,000 store employees and 60 corporate staff lost their jobs.

By then the chain was down to six stores taking in roughly $170 million a year, against $230 million from 23 stores at the 2002 peak, according to The Hollywood Reporter.

Simon Wright, then chief executive of Virgin Entertainment Group, told TheWrap in 2009: "It's sad news, but it has nothing to do with the stores." He was making the same point Mathrani had made from the other side of the table. The retailer worked. The math on the lease did not.

The timing was brutal for anyone hoping a buyer would appear. In the spring of 2009 nobody was starting a national music chain, and the format itself was going the way of the mall record shop.

Is the Virgin Megastore brand still trading anywhere?

Yes, but not in North America, and not under Virgin's own management.

Virgin Group licenses the Virgin Megastore name to regional operators. The surviving chain is in the Middle East and North Africa, run by the Beirut-based retail group Azadea, and the company dates its first regional store to the UAE in 2001.

Virgin Megastore's own site says the chain now runs more than 40 stores across nine markets in the region, selling technology, games, books, music and lifestyle goods rather than acting as a record shop.

So the answer to who owns Virgin Megastore now has two halves. Virgin Group owns the trademark. Azadea owns the stores that carry it, and there is no US location and no announced plan for one.

For an American reader, that is the practical status in 2026: the brand exists, the shopping bags exist, and the nearest store is thousands of miles away.

What sits in the Times Square space now

The 1540 Broadway retail space did not stay empty for long. Forever 21 moved into the former Virgin Megastore floors in 2010 and turned them into one of the largest fast-fashion stores in Manhattan.

That tenant has since wound down its own US business, which makes the block a short history of two retail formats that both looked unbeatable and both ran out of road.

The lesson the property companies drew in 2007 held up anyway. The building kept earning. The tenants kept changing.

Where to find one today

There is no Virgin Megastore to walk into in the United States, so what circulates is memorabilia: branded carrier bags from the New York, London and Paris stores, staff shirts, in-store promo posters and listening-post signage. Condition drives the price, and anything tied to the Times Square store asks the most. Etsy is the sensible place to look, because sellers there date and photograph the pieces: browse Virgin Megastore memorabilia on Etsy.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Virgin Megastore still around in 2026?

Virgin Megastore still exists in 2026, but not in the United States. The chain's own site says it operates more than 40 stores across nine markets in the Middle East and North Africa, while the last American Virgin Megastore closed in the summer of 2009.

When did Virgin Megastore close in the US?

Virgin Megastore closed its remaining six US stores between April and June 2009. The Times Square flagship in New York shut in mid-April 2009, and the stores in Los Angeles, San Francisco, Orlando and Denver followed by June, costing about 1,000 store jobs and 60 corporate ones.

Who owns Virgin Megastore now?
