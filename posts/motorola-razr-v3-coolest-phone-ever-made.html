Does the Motorola Razr V3 Still Work in 2026?

2026-04-01 | Updated 2026-09-25 | By Marcus Vale | 5 min read

The Motorola Razr V3 no longer works as a phone in the United States. It was a 2G-only GSM handset, and T-Mobile switched off the last major US 2G network shortly after 9 a.m. Eastern on August 3, 2026, after AT&T dropped 2G on January 1, 2017 and Verizon followed in 2020. The Razr name still exists under Lenovo, which sells folding Razrs from $799 to $1,899 in 2026, while original V3s trade on Etsy from about $65.

Why can't a Razr V3 make a call anymore?

The V3 is a quad-band GSM handset with a 2G radio and nothing else. It depends on networks that no major US carrier still operates.

AT&T switched its 2G GSM network off on January 1, 2017, which also ended cellular service for the original iPhone. Verizon followed in 2020. T-Mobile held on the longest and then cut the last major US 2G signal shortly after 9 a.m. Eastern on August 3, 2026.

A T-Mobile spokesperson gave Fierce Network the reason for the delay in 2026: "T-Mobile retained GSM longer than other carriers to give customers and partners additional time to migrate legacy devices." Those devices were alarm panels, trackers and the handsets of international roamers who arrived without VoLTE, not collectors' flip phones.

So a V3 in 2026 powers on, lights the etched keypad and snaps shut with the same sound, and cannot register on a network. No calls, no texts, no 911. It is hardware that outlived its own infrastructure, the way the pay phones still standing in the United States did.

How many Razr V3s did Motorola sell?

About 130 million across every V3 variant over roughly four years of production. Motorola never published an audited lifetime total, so that figure is a tally assembled afterwards and repeated by the tech press. Treat it as the accepted number rather than a company-certified one.

The milestones on the way up are firmer, because Motorola announced them. The company said it had shipped its 50 millionth Razr V3 on July 18, 2006. The 100 millionth Razr came with second-quarter results on July 19, 2007, almost exactly a year later.

The US run was the remarkable part. The V3 and its variants were the best-selling handset in the United States for 12 consecutive quarters, which is three straight years at number one. No clamshell has come near that total since, and the phone that finally took the crown was not another flip phone.

Why did a $499 flip phone sell like that?

Motorola launched the V3 in the United States on November 16, 2004, exclusively through Cingular Wireless, at $499 with a two-year contract. That made it the most expensive phone in Motorola's range, and the exclusivity meant anyone on another carrier had to wait or switch.

The body justified the price. The V3 measured 13.9 millimeters thick and weighed 95 grams, in a market full of plastic bricks. The chassis was anodized aluminum. The keypad was a flat etched metal sheet rather than rubber nubs, backlit so the digits glowed through it.

Motorola knew it was selling an object rather than a telephone. Chief executive Ed Zander called it "the device formerly known as the cell phone" in Chicago Magazine in December 2005, and told the same magazine that the last thing "you're going to do on this device five years from now is make a phone call."

The trade-offs were real: a 0.3-megapixel VGA camera, minimal storage, no 3G radio. None of it mattered to buyers, in the same way the Nokia 3310 sold on how much abuse it survived rather than on its specifications.

What ended the original Razr's run?

Success, mostly. Motorola spent the back half of the decade re-releasing the same phone. The V3i added a better camera and a memory card slot. The V3x and V3xx bolted on 3G. The Razr2 arrived in 2007 as the actual sequel, three years after the original, with the same hinge and the same silhouette.

The rest of the industry moved to software while that was happening. BlackBerry owned corporate email. Nokia pushed Symbian. Apple shipped the iPhone in 2007, and the iPhone 3G overtook the Razr as the best-selling US handset in the third quarter of 2008, ending that 12-quarter streak.

The V3 was discontinued that same year, after selling about 3 million units in its final quarter on the market. Motorola had built the most popular phone in America with nothing queued behind it, and its handset division spent the following years shrinking into a takeover target.

Who owns the Razr name today?

Lenovo. Google, which had bought Motorola Mobility in 2012, sold the handset business to Lenovo for $2.91 billion in cash and stock, a deal that closed on October 30, 2014 with Google keeping about a 6 percent stake and most of the patents.

Lenovo brought the name back in November 2019 as a vertically folding smartphone, priced at $1,499 and sold in the US through Verizon, with pre-orders opening December 26, 2019. Prices dropped as folding hinges got cheaper to build, and then went back up.

The 2026 lineup, announced on April 29, 2026, with pre-orders on May 14 and shipping on May 21, runs $799 for the Razr, $1,099 for the Razr+, $1,499 for the Razr Ultra and $1,899 for the book-style Razr Fold. The Ultra costs exactly what the first folding Razr cost in 2019. Reviewers noted the 2026 phones are close to spec-identical to the 2025 ones.

Where to find one today

Original V3s are still easy to find and still cheap. Checked in September 2026, listings on Etsy's Motorola Razr V3 search included an untested magenta handset at $65, with common black and silver examples in the same double-digit range and rare colors asking far more: one silver V3 was marked at $446.25, down from $595.

Two things to watch. Many listings are described as untested or seller refurbished, which usually means a replacement shell or a fresh battery rather than an untouched 2004 unit, so read the description if originality matters. And nothing you buy will make a call in the US, whatever the listing says about being unlocked, because the network it needs is gone.

Complete boxes with the charger and manual carry most of the premium. A loose handset with a tired battery is a desk ornament, and for most buyers in 2026 that is the point.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the Motorola Razr V3 still work in 2026?

The Motorola Razr V3 powers on and its hardware still works, but it cannot connect to a US carrier. The V3 is 2G GSM only, and the last major US 2G network, T-Mobile's, was shut off on August 3, 2026, after AT&T dropped 2G on January 1, 2017 and Verizon in 2020.

How much is a Motorola Razr V3 worth in 2026?

Original Motorola Razr V3 handsets listed on Etsy in September 2026 started around $65 for an untested phone, with rare colors asking into the hundreds, including one silver V3 at $446.25. Condition, color and a complete box set the price, since none of them work as phones in the United States anymore.

Who owns Motorola now?

Lenovo owns Motorola's phone business, having bought it from Google for $2.91 billion in a deal that closed on October 30, 2014. Lenovo sells the modern folding Razr line, which in 2026 runs from $799 for the base Razr to $1,899 for the Razr Fold.
