Where can you read old Sears Wish Books for free?

Three free archives hold most of what survives in scanned form, and none of them belongs to Sears. The company's own history site, searsarchives.com, now just forwards visitors to sears.com.

The Internet Archive is the deepest. Its search returns at least one upload for 1985, 1986, 1988, 1989 and 1991, often two, and its 1970s bundle alone holds 2.5 GB of scans with 20,553 views since it went up in August 2021.

WishbookWeb is the oldest. Collectors started it in 2006, scanning whole catalogs rather than favorite toy pages, which is why it still feeds many of the uploads elsewhere. Knowing where to look is half the job; the other half is knowing which year to open.

Which year is the Wish Book you remember?

The cover rarely helps, but the toys inside date a copy fast. Seller descriptions from November 2026 make a handy guide to the 1980s and early 1990s editions, the ones most readers of this site grew up circling in pen.

  • 1983: a 611-page book with Coleco, Atari and BMX bikes.
  • 1984: Transformers, Atari, Cabbage Patch Kids and Lego.
  • 1985: G.I. Joe, Cabbage Patch Kids, Rainbow Brite and My Little Pony.
  • 1989: a 669-page edition sold as the Great American Wish Book.
  • 1990: Nintendo, Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles and Batman.
  • 1992: Game Boy, Sega Genesis and the Turtles again.

The name on the cover is a clue too. Listings from 1989 to 1992 mostly read "Great American Wish Book," while late 1990s copies are thinner holiday booklets, one 1998 edition running 287 pages.

That shrinking page count is the real story of the catalog. At its peak the Wish Book ran to more than 800 pages, so how did it end up as a web page?

The 120-page comeback mailed out in 2017

Sears first mailed a holiday catalog in 1933, and the early editions ran under 100 pages. By the peak years the book topped 800 pages, then it slimmed down through the mid-1990s and was nearly gone by the early 2000s, as Antique Trader recounted in 2018.

Smaller holiday editions kept appearing until 2011. Then, on October 25, 2017, Sears announced a 120-page printed Wish Book, mailed to its best Shop Your Way members, with others invited to pick up a copy in store while supplies lasted. "Our members told us they missed the Wish Book, so we had to bring it back," chief marketing officer Kelly Cook said in the company's announcement.

The comeback lasted one season. In October 2018, Business Insider reported that the Wish Book would appear only online that year, the same month Sears filed for bankruptcy. No printed edition has followed, and what happened to the company afterward is its own story, told in who owns Sears now.

Its rivals went first. Montgomery Ward and JCPenney had their own Christmas books, and today they share archive space with Sears on the fan sites. That leaves the paper copies, and the question every attic clean-out raises: is one worth keeping?

What old Sears Wish Books sell for

A Wish Book from the 1980s usually asks $75 to $150, and a 1990s copy $30 to $60. Older books sell for more, though the most recent dated sold prices for them come from a 2018 roundup, so treat those rows as a floor.

Contents drive the price more than age. Toy-heavy books with dolls, trains and Buddy L trucks draw the most interest, and "It depends on what they played with as a child," Miles Clark told Antique Trader in 2018. Collector Jason Liebig put the appeal more simply in the same piece: "Wish Books are such a great window into the past."

Wear is what kills a price: torn covers, clipped pages and circled toys that someone cut out. An unopened copy or one with its order form still inside asks the top of the range, the same premium for completeness that runs through old Nintendo Power magazines.

Where to find one today: paper copies turn up at estate sales, used bookstores and on Etsy, where vintage Wish Books are listed every holiday season. For reading rather than owning, the free scans do the job, and they never shed pages. For most people the scan is the better gift: the toys they wanted in 1985, one click away, still on the page where they left them.

Frequently Asked Questions

When was the last Sears Wish Book printed?

The last printed Sears Wish Book was the 120-page 2017 edition, mailed to Sears' best Shop Your Way members that October. In 2018 the Wish Book appeared only online, and Sears has not printed one since.

Are old Sears Wish Books worth anything?

Yes, a little. In November 2026 sellers asked $75 to $150 for 1980s Sears Wish Books and $30 to $60 for early 1990s copies, while a 1969 edition in its original mailing sleeve sold for $173 in 2018.

Can you read the 1985 Sears Wish Book online?

Yes. The Internet Archive hosts free uploads titled 1985 Sears Christmas Wishbook Catalog and 1985 Sears Christmas Wish Book, which can be read in the browser or downloaded. WishbookWeb's second site covers 1976 and later editions as well.
