When did the Razr V3 come out, and what did it cost?

The Motorola Razr V3 went on sale in the United States on November 16, 2004, through Cingular Wireless and nobody else, at $499 with a two-year contract. Motorola had shown it almost four months earlier, on July 27, 2004, at a company event in Chicago.

Coverage from the time rounds the launch figure two ways. NBC News put it at about $500 and called the V3 the star of a portfolio of 20 new Motorola handsets. RCR Wireless, looking back in 2006, recorded Cingular's opening offer as $500. Either way it was the most expensive phone in Motorola's range that season, and anyone on another carrier had to switch providers to get one.

What people forget is how fast that number fell. RCR Wireless traced the slide in an article on October 30, 2006, reporting that Cingular had "cut the price to $400, then to $350, and down to $200" by May 2005. After the holidays that season, Cingular and T-Mobile USA went to $150 while Alltel and Verizon Wireless held at $200, and by September 2006 T-Mobile was selling the V3 at $50.

So the honest answer to "what did it cost" depends on the month: $500 at launch, $200 seven months later, $50 two years in. The collapse is also why roughly 130 million of them exist, and why so many are still in drawers. What those drawers hold is a spec sheet that reads strangely now.

Motorola Razr V3 specs: what the 2004 hardware actually did

The V3 was a quad-band GSM phone with a 2.2 inch screen, a VGA camera and about 5.5 MB of storage that could not be expanded. GSMArena, Phone Scoop and Softpedia agree on the sheet, differing only on whether the memory is 5.5 or 5.6 MB.

There is no app store on that list, no keyboard and no way to add software, and in 2004 nobody minded. The phone sold on how it looked, the way the Nokia 3310 sold on how much abuse it survived. Motorola's answer to every complaint that followed was another version of the same phone.

The difference collectors ask about most is the Razr V3 against the Razr V3i, and it comes down to three things. The V3i has a 1.23 megapixel camera instead of the V3's VGA unit, a microSD slot the V3 does not have at all, and, on some carrier versions, Apple's iTunes software with a hard ceiling of 50 or 100 songs depending on the region.

Everything else is near enough identical: same hinge, same etched keypad, same 2.2 inch screen, 5 grams heavier at 100 grams. A V3i in good condition is the better phone of the two and usually the cheaper one, because far fewer people are sentimental about it.

Can you use a Motorola Razr V3 in 2026?

Not as a phone in the United States. Every original V3 is 2G GSM only, AT&T switched its 2G network off in 2017, Verizon Wireless followed in 2020, and T-Mobile shut down the last major US 2G GSM network on August 3, 2026.

Buying an unlocked one changes nothing here, and that is the single most expensive misunderstanding in the listings. Unlocked means no carrier lock in the software. It does not add a radio, and the radio is the problem: there is no US network left for a 2G handset to register on, so an unlocked V3 cannot place a call, send a text or dial 911.

A T-Mobile spokesperson told Fierce Network in 2026 that the remaining 2G user base was "de minimis" and that "Any remaining customers have been informed and provided ample time to migrate to modern network technologies." What was still on that network by the end was alarm panels, utility meters and logistics trackers, not collectors' flip phones.

Abroad is a different and shrinking story. A quad-band V3 can still find a 2G signal in some markets, but the switch-offs are rolling: Orange is retiring 2G across mainland France through 2026, Bouygues and SFR have said they will follow by the end of that year, and Deutsche Telekom has set June 30, 2028 for Germany. Everything else about the phone still works. It charges, it lights the keypad, it snaps shut with the same sound, and it has done since it was the best selling phone in America.

Twelve straight quarters at No. 1, and the quarter it lost

The Razr led The NPD Group's ranking of handsets bought by US adults for twelve consecutive quarters, three full years at number one, until the third quarter of 2008.

NPD's figures for that quarter, published on November 10, 2008 and reported by Engadget, put the top five as the iPhone 3G, the Motorola Razr V3, the BlackBerry Curve, the LG Rumor and the LG enV2. Ross Rubin, NPD's director of industry analysis, called it a "watershed shift in handset design from fashion to fashionable functionality." Phones with QWERTY keyboards had climbed to 30 percent of US sales from 11 percent a year earlier.

Buyers had started paying for keyboards and software, and the V3 offered neither. BlackBerry owned corporate email by then, and Apple had made a touchscreen the thing to beat.

The bill came due fast. Motorola's handset business was losing money by the end of 2007, in March 2008 the company said it would separate its mobile devices unit into a standalone public company, and the V3 line was discontinued that year. Motorola never published an audited lifetime total for it, so the roughly 130 million figure repeated by the tech press is a tally assembled afterwards rather than a certified number. It was still enough to make the V3 the best selling clamshell phone ever built, which left the name worth more than the company that made it.

Who owns the Razr name today?

Lenovo. Google, which had bought Motorola Mobility for $12.5 billion in 2012, sold the handset business on in a deal that closed on October 30, 2014 at a purchase price of about $2.91 billion: roughly $660 million in cash, $750 million in Lenovo shares and a $1.5 billion three-year promissory note.

Google kept the majority of the patent portfolio. Motorola went to Lenovo with the brand, about 3,500 employees, its Chicago headquarters and more than 2,000 patent assets. "This partnership has always been a perfect fit," Lenovo chairman and chief executive Yang Yuanqing said in the announcement that day.

Lenovo brought the name back in 2019 as a vertically folding smartphone and has shipped a new one almost every year since. The 2026 line was announced on April 29, 2026, with pre-orders on May 14 and shipping on May 21, at $799 for the Razr, $1,099 for the Razr+, $1,499 for the Razr Ultra and $1,899 for the book-style Razr Fold. 9to5Google reported that the 2026 phones are close to spec-identical to the 2025 ones at higher prices, which is a familiar move from the company that answered the V3 with the V3i.

What a Razr V3 is worth now, and where to find one

An original V3 trades on condition and packaging, and the spread between a parts handset and a sealed one is the entire market. No public price database tracks phones the way PriceCharting tracks games, so the figures below are asking prices gathered from one marketplace on one date, not sold prices, and a seller can ask anything.

ConditionAsking price (Etsy, November 2026)What to check first

Read the listing text before paying for the top of that range. Many are described as untested or seller refurbished, which usually means a replacement shell or a fresh battery rather than an untouched 2004 unit, and a refurbished V3 is worth considerably less than a sealed one to anyone who collects them.

The Etsy Motorola Razr V3 search is the practical place to look, and the same search is worth repeating for the V3i, which costs less and takes better photos. Expect a loose handset at the bottom of the range and a boxed one near the top.

Whatever a listing says about being unlocked, nothing bought in 2026 will make a call in the United States, because the network it needs no longer exists. That puts the V3 in the same drawer as the Motorola StarTAC that cost $1,000 in 1996: beautiful hardware that outlived its own infrastructure, and worth keeping anyway.

Frequently Asked Questions

What was the Motorola Razr V3's original price?

The Motorola Razr V3 launched in the United States in November 2004 at $499 with a two-year Cingular Wireless contract, reported by some outlets as $500. RCR Wireless recorded Cingular cutting it to $400, then $350, then $200 by May 2005, and by September 2006 T-Mobile USA was selling the V3 at $50.

Can you still use a Motorola Razr V3 in 2026?

Not in the United States. The Razr V3 is a 2G GSM handset, and the last major US 2G GSM network, T-Mobile's, was switched off on August 3, 2026, after AT&T ended its own in 2017 and Verizon Wireless followed in 2020. An unlocked V3 is no help, because unlocking removes a carrier lock and does not add a radio the networks still support.

What is the difference between the Motorola Razr V3 and the V3i?

The Razr V3i, announced in November 2005, upgraded the V3's VGA camera to 1.23 megapixels, added a microSD card slot for cards up to 512 MB, and shipped with Apple's iTunes software on some carrier versions, capped at 50 or 100 songs by region. It is 5 grams heavier at 100 grams and otherwise near identical, and Cingular sold it at $249.99 on a two-year contract in September 2006.
