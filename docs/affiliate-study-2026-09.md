# Affiliate program study for 404memoryfound.com

Nostalgia blog covering 1990s and 2000s technology, games, websites and companies. Audience: US first, then UK, Canada, Australia. Readers 25 to 45 with disposable income. Post types: status posts ("is X still around", "who owns X now"), product histories (consoles, handhelds, phones, PCs, toys), artifact posts (sounds, mascots, logos).

Research date: 9 September 2026. Constraint honored: eBay Partner Network excluded throughout. Amazon Associates is covered as a baseline only.

**Verification convention used here.** Where a rate came from the merchant's own page or its own network profile, the source URL is a merchant or network domain. Where the only source was a third-party affiliate directory (getlasso, uppromote, linkclicky, taprefer, flexoffers, affi.io and similar), the row is marked **(aggregator, unverified)**. Those directories are frequently stale and frequently wrong, so treat them as a lead to verify inside the network dashboard, not as a fact. Where no rate could be found at all, the row says **not published**. Nothing in this document is a guess.

---

## (a) Executive summary: the top 10 to apply to first

Ranked for this specific site, weighting three things: revenue per outbound click (commission percent times realistic order value), whether a zero-traffic site can actually get approved today, and how many existing post types the program monetizes.

1. **Legacybox / Southtree / Kodak Digitizing (one company, AMB Media) via ShareASale/Awin.** Up to 10 percent, 30-day cookie. Order values run in the hundreds of dollars because customers ship boxes of tapes. This is the single best revenue-per-click fit: every "whatever happened to VHS/Hi8/MiniDV" and camcorder history post ends naturally at "and here is how you get yours off tape." Monetizes: artifact posts, format-death posts, camcorder and home-video histories.
2. **ScanMyPhotos via Awin and ShareASale.** Commissions stated by the merchant as starting at 10 percent and reaching up to 30 percent with performance and seasonal incentives. Same reader intent as Legacybox, different media type (prints, slides, negatives). Monetizes: photography, disposable camera, 1-hour photo lab, and family-archive posts.
3. **Etsy via Awin.** 4 percent for content publishers, 30-day web cookie (7-day in-app). This is the practical eBay replacement for this site. Etsy is where the actual 90s and 2000s artifacts live: Furby, Beanie Babies, Tamagotchi, mall-store merch, Lisa Frank, dead-brand tees, CRT-era ephemera. Monetizes: nearly every artifact and toy post on the site.
4. **Stone Age Gamer (direct).** 2.5 percent baseline with no traffic minimum, plus published creator tiers that require a 3-month-old site and go higher. Editorially perfect: flash carts, repro cartridges, region adapters, console mods. Monetizes: console histories, "can you still play X" status posts.
5. **Anbernic via UpPromote.** 2 percent base, 6 percent after profile review, 30-day cookie, 10 USD payout threshold, PayPal. Explicitly built for small creators with a blog or channel, so a new site can get in. Monetizes: modern-retro handheld posts, "the Game Boy successor that isn't Nintendo" angle, emulation explainers.
6. **GOG (direct, own affiliate platform).** 6 percent flat on net sales, 7-day cookie, program is open to everyone including small blogs. Uniquely on-thesis: GOG's whole business is making 90s and 2000s PC games run today, which is literally what a "is X still playable" post concludes. Monetizes: PC game histories, abandonware and preservation posts.
7. **Humble Bundle via Impact.** 15 percent on bundles, 5 percent on Humble Store, 7-day cookie, 25 USD payout threshold, monthly via Impact. Retro PC catalogs rotate through bundles constantly. Monetizes: PC game and publisher-history posts.
8. **Displate.** Reported 25 percent per sale, 30-day cookie **(aggregator, unverified: confirm inside the program before quoting it)**. Very high margin per order for a low-friction impulse product. Monetizes: mascot posts, logo and brand-identity posts, artifact posts where the payoff is a wall print.
9. **Walmart via Impact / Walmart Creator.** Broad catalog covering consoles, toys, turntables, instant cameras. Rates are set by Walmart's own fee schedule rather than a public rate card. Note the gate: Walmart Creator requires US residence and 1,000 followers across connected social accounts. Monetizes: product-history posts where the modern reissue is in stock at a big-box retailer.
10. **Green Man Gaming via Impact.** Up to 5 percent per sale, 10 percent on GMG bundles, per the merchant's own affiliate page. Cookie window is not published on that page. Second digital-games leg alongside GOG, useful when a title is Steam-key only. Monetizes: PC game histories.

**Deliberately not in the top 10, and why.** Amazon Associates has the widest catalog but pays 1 to 3 percent on exactly the categories this site writes about (video games 2 percent, electronics 2.5 percent, toys 3 percent) on a 24-hour cookie, and it will close a new account that fails to produce 3 qualifying orders in 180 days. Run it as a fallback link layer, not as the plan. Best Buy (roughly 1 percent, 1-day cookie) and GameStop (up to 5 percent but a 1-day cookie) both waste the long consideration cycle a nostalgia reader has. Target pays 0 percent on electronics and toys, which is most of what this site would link.

## (c) Revenue model at 10k, 30k and 50k monthly visits

### Assumptions, stated plainly

Every number below is derived from the assumptions in this block. Change an assumption and the output changes proportionally. These are modelling assumptions, not published figures.

**Traffic and ads.**
- 1 visit is treated as 1 session. Pageviews per session assumed at 1.4, which is typical for a blog where most entries are single-article search landings.
- At 10k and 30k: Journey by Mediavine, session RPM assumed **10 to 16 USD**, planning at 12. Basis: the 12.69 USD RPM reported by a real Journey site over 90 days, adjusted for this site's Tier 1 US-heavy audience (which helps) against the 2026 display-market softness (which hurts).
- At 50k sessions (70k pageviews): Raptive-eligible, session RPM assumed **14 to 22 USD**, planning at 18.
- Ad revenue is highly seasonal. Q4 can run 40 to 60 percent above the annual average and Q1 well below it. The figures below are annual averages, not December.

**Affiliate.**
- Affiliate click-through rate: **3 percent of visits** click an outbound affiliate link. This assumes links are placed at intent moments (end of a product history, inside a "can you still get one" section), not in a sidebar.
- Conversion on those clicks: **3 percent** blended. Short-cookie merchants (Amazon 24h, GOG 7d, LEGO 7d) drag this down; long-cookie high-consideration merchants (Legacybox 30d, Etsy 30d) pull it up.
- Blended commission per converted order: **6.50 USD**. Built from the actual mix this site can realistically drive: Amazon at roughly 60 USD order value times 2.5 percent equals 1.50 USD; Etsy at 45 USD times 4 percent equals 1.80 USD; a handheld at 150 USD times 6 percent equals 9 USD; Legacybox at 180 USD times 10 percent equals 18 USD; Displate at 60 USD times 25 percent equals 15 USD. Weighted toward the cheap end because Amazon and Etsy will be the majority of clicks by volume.
- Affiliate performance is assumed to improve slightly with scale as the site learns which merchants convert, hence the rising rates below.

### The model

| | 10,000 visits/mo | 30,000 visits/mo | 50,000 visits/mo |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ad network | Journey by Mediavine | Journey by Mediavine | Raptive |
| Session RPM (planning) | 12 USD | 12 USD | 18 USD |
| **Ad revenue** | **120 USD** (range 100 to 160) | **360 USD** (range 300 to 480) | **900 USD** (range 700 to 1,100) |
| Affiliate outbound clicks (3%) | 300 | 900 | 1,750 (3.5% at scale) |
| Converted orders (3%) | 9 | 27 | 61 (3.5% at scale) |
| Commission per order | 6.50 USD | 6.50 USD | 7.00 USD |
| **Affiliate revenue** | **59 USD** (range 30 to 110) | **176 USD** (range 90 to 330) | **428 USD** (range 220 to 800) |

| Affiliate share of total | 33% | 33% | 32% |

### The honest conclusion about the 5,000 USD target

**50,000 monthly visits does not reach 5,000 USD per month on ads plus affiliate.** On these assumptions it reaches roughly 1,300 USD, or about 1,900 USD in a good month. Getting to 5,000 USD on this revenue mix alone requires somewhere in the region of **170,000 to 200,000 monthly visits**, which is three to four times the stated 12-month traffic goal.

That is not a reason to abandon the target. It is a reason to be explicit that the target implies one of three things:

1. **More traffic than planned.** 50k in year one, 200k by year two or three. Realistic for an evergreen nostalgia site with strong search intent, but it is a 24 to 36 month path, not 12 to 24.
2. **A higher-value revenue mix.** Digitizing services alone can move the blended commission per order from 6.50 USD to 15 USD or more if the site builds a dedicated "get your tapes off tape" content cluster that sends a disproportionate share of clicks to Legacybox and ScanMyPhotos. Doing that lifts the 50k figure toward 2,000 to 2,500 USD.
3. **A third revenue line that is not ads or affiliate.** An email list, a sponsor slot sold directly to a retro hardware brand, or a small product (a print, a zine, a nostalgia-quiz app). At 50k monthly visits a list of 5,000 to 8,000 subscribers is achievable, and a directly sold newsletter sponsorship in this niche is worth far more per reader than programmatic display.

The realistic read: **ads plus affiliate gets this site to roughly 1,000 to 2,000 USD per month at 50k visits.** The remaining 3,000 USD has to come from either much more traffic or a fourth revenue line.

## (e) Risks

**1. Amazon's 180-day rule will close a premature account.** An Associates account that has not referred **3 qualifying purchases within 180 days of signup** is closed. Three qualifying sales means three separate orders, not three items in one order, and they must not be returned inside the return window. Amazon states it cannot reinstate a closed account or the Associates ID; the only route back is a fresh application once the site is established. **Mitigation:** do not apply until there is real traffic. On a 24-hour cookie at 2 to 3 percent, Amazon is a convenience layer, not a business, so there is no cost to waiting.
Source: https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/node/topic/G7MJTPEP9NC3YKMG

**2. Rates and programs change or vanish without notice.** Amazon sets its category rates unilaterally and has cut them repeatedly. Target ended its entire Raptive affiliate channel in January 2026. Ezoic closed its doors to sites under 250,000 users in February 2026. Redbubble's real published rate (up to 2 percent) is a fraction of what affiliate directories still claim (10 percent). **Mitigation:** never hard-code a commission percentage into post copy, verify every rate inside the network dashboard before writing about it, and do not let any single merchant exceed roughly 40 percent of affiliate revenue.

**3. The independents in this niche mostly have no program at all.** DKOldies, Lukie Games, JJGames, Castlemania, Limited Run Games, Analogue, Hyperkin, Evercade, Antstream, Nintendo and Discogs all publish nothing. That is a large share of the merchants a retro post would most naturally link. **Mitigation:** design post templates so the monetized link is a category the site can actually get paid on (digitizing, Etsy, modern-retro handhelds, digital storefronts) rather than the specific vendor the article is about.

**4. FTC disclosure is a live legal exposure, not a formality.** The governing document is the FTC's Guides Concerning the Use of Endorsements and Testimonials in Advertising (16 CFR Part 255), last updated June 2023. The requirement is a clear and conspicuous disclosure **in the same post as the affiliate link and placed before the reader engages with it**. A sitewide disclaimer page or a footer line does not satisfy it. Reported penalties reach into the tens of thousands of dollars per violation. **Mitigation:** a short disclosure line rendered above the first affiliate link in every post template, not a link to a policy page. Given the site is a static build, this belongs in the layout, not in each post's body.
Sources: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking ; https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-255

**5. UK and EU readers bring cookie consent obligations that directly cut affiliate revenue.** Affiliate tracking cookies are not strictly necessary cookies, so under GDPR and the ePrivacy Directive they require prior opt-in consent from EU and UK visitors. Readers who decline are not tracked and their purchases do not pay. With the UK explicitly in this site's target audience, expect a measurable share of clicks from that market to be untracked. **Mitigation:** run a consent banner that is honest rather than clever, model UK and EU traffic at a lower effective conversion rate than US traffic, and prefer merchants offering server-side or coupon-code attribution where available (Anbernic, for instance, credits both link clicks and coupon-code use).

**6. Concentration risk on the digitizing category.** Legacybox, Southtree and Kodak Digitizing are one company (AMB Media). Recommending all three is not diversification, it is one merchant relationship wearing three logos. The service has also drawn public criticism over turnaround and quality, which is a credibility risk for a site whose entire value is being trusted about the past. **Mitigation:** pair it with ScanMyPhotos as a genuinely separate operator, and only recommend what the site would recommend unpaid.

**7. The 2026 display-ad environment.** Publishers across the US, Europe and India reported AdSense RPM drops of 30 to 90 percent since January 2026 as AI answers absorbed search clicks. This affects the traffic assumption as much as the RPM assumption: a site built on "is X still around" queries is precisely the kind of informational search that AI overviews answer without a click. **Mitigation:** weight the plan toward affiliate and toward an owned email list rather than toward search-dependent display revenue, and build post formats that give a reason to click through (galleries, archives, interactive artifacts) rather than a paragraph an AI summary can replace.
Source: https://advantrise.com/adsense-in-2026-rpms-crashed-ai-took-the-clicks-and-publishers-are-leaving
