Is TiVo Still Around? What Your Old TiVo Can Still Do

By Theo Lindqvist · Updated November 21, 2026 · First published March 27, 2026 · 9 min read

TiVo is still around in 2026, but as a brand of Xperi Inc. that sells TiVo OS software for smart TVs, not as a maker of recorders. TiVo stopped selling DVRs on October 1, 2025, yet it still serves the Roamio, Bolt and Edge boxes in people's homes, where a new activation now costs $14.99 a month and lifetime plans already paid for keep working.

What TiVo stopped selling on October 1, 2025

TiVo stopped selling DVRs and DVR accessories on October 1, 2025, after quietly pulling its last recorders from its website. "TiVo no longer manufactures hardware, and our remaining inventory is now depleted, though we will continue to offer support for the products going forward," the company told Variety that October.

That closed 26 years of TiVo hardware. The first TiVo, built by Philips, went on sale on March 31, 1999, at $499 for a 14-hour model or $999 for 30 hours, and the service that fed it a program guide cost $9.95 a month or $199 for life. The pitch was pausing live TV and never again wrestling with the timer on a VCR.

The decline started long before 2025. On January 31, 2006, TiVo counted about 4.4 million subscriptions, but 2,873,000 of them were DirecTV satellite receivers running TiVo software, against 1,491,000 boxes people had bought from TiVo itself. Cable and satellite companies went on to build recording into the set-top boxes they already rented out, and streaming services such as Netflix later made recording broadcast TV unnecessary for most households.

"We are very proud of the TiVo DVR legacy," the company said in the same 2025 statement. For the boxes still plugged into televisions, the practical question is which ones TiVo still serves.

Which TiVo models can still be activated in 2026?

Every TiVo DVR depends on TiVo's service for its guide and its scheduled recordings, and TiVo's support site now splits the lineup in two. A Roamio, Bolt or Edge can still be activated on a new plan. Series 1, Series 2, Series 3, the Series 4 Premiere and the first TiVo Mini and Mini 2 cannot.

The older boxes have not been switched off. TiVo says Series 2, Series 3 and Premiere DVRs already activated with Product Lifetime Service "will continue to receive service." The catch sits in the same notice: cancel the plan on one of those boxes and it can never be reactivated.

Series 1 owners have already seen that ending. TiVo cut their service on September 29, 2016, saying the boxes "will not operate with current software versions and will no longer be supported by the TiVo service," Engadget reported that August. About 3,500 were still active, and each owner was offered a $75 prepaid Visa card. A Series 1 still shows live TV and plays old recordings but cannot record from a guide, which makes the cost of keeping a newer box connected the next thing to check.

What it costs to keep a TiVo running now

For a newly activated Roamio, Bolt or Edge, TiVo lists exactly one plan in 2026: $14.99 a month, with no commitment and a 30-day money-back guarantee. That includes the Edge for antenna, which launched in 2019 with a $6.99 monthly plan. The TiVo Mini VOX and Mini LUX need no plan of their own.

A box that already has lifetime service, which TiVo sold in its last years as the All-In Plan, keeps it, and the plan can move to a new owner. The original buyer submits a Transfer Ownership Authorization Form with the sales order number, which typically begins with TS or AS, then gives the new owner the case number and the box's TiVo Service Number (TSN). A monthly plan does not travel; the seller has to cancel it before the buyer can activate.

That makes the plan worth more than the hardware. Used Roamios, Bolts and spare remotes still turn up on Etsy, and before paying for one it is worth asking whether the seller can authorize a lifetime transfer, because without one the box costs $14.99 a month from the first day. Even fully paid boxes lost a favorite feature on November 2, 2026.

The November 2, 2026 change that put SkipMode behind a $2.99 fee

SkipMode arrived with the Bolt in September 2015. TiVo marked where the breaks fell on "the top 20 or so most popular" networks between 4 p.m. and midnight, Engadget reported at the time, and one press of a button jumped an entire commercial break.

In a September 2026 email, TiVo told owners that the Skip Button version of SkipMode would stop working on November 2, 2026. "We are also excited to share that in November we will be testing a new Premium Auto Commercial Skip add-on service," the email continued, as quoted by the gadget blog Zatz Not Funny on September 4, 2026.

The replacement now sits on TiVo's support site as Premium Commercial Skip, priced at $2.99 a month. It covers Edge, Bolt, Roamio and Mini boxes running TiVo Experience 3 or 4, began with a 30-day free trial in early November that needed no sign-up, and is switched on one DVR at a time under My Account, then Manage Devices. A Mini is covered by the DVR it connects to.

TiVo pitches the paid version as wider than the old one, reaching sports, news, reality shows and talk shows as well as prime time. It is also not the first time the money in TiVo has come from the technology rather than from the boxes.

How TiVo's patents earned it over $1 billion

The asset that kept TiVo valuable through its lean years was a stack of patents, led by US Patent No. 6,233,389, which TiVo called its Time Warp patent. TiVo took the companies selling rival DVRs to court, and the settlements landed between 2011 and 2013.

Dish Network and EchoStar settled first. On May 2, 2011, they agreed to pay TiVo $500 million, $300 million up front and the remaining $200 million in six equal yearly installments from 2012 through 2017, for a license to the Time Warp patent for the rest of its life. Dish Network chairman and chief executive Charlie Ergen said the companies were "pleased to put this litigation behind us and move forward."

AT&T followed with a $215 million settlement in TiVo's fiscal year that ended January 31, 2012, and Verizon paid $250.4 million in the year that ended January 31, 2013. Those three deals alone come to $965.4 million, and the annual report TiVo filed in 2013 said its patents had "generated over $1 billion in damages and technology revenue consideration."

Patents that profitable attract buyers, and in 2016 one arrived.

Who owns TiVo now, Xperi or Adeia?

Xperi Inc. owns TiVo. The brand sits in TiVo Platform Technologies LLC, a wholly owned subsidiary of Xperi, which trades on the New York Stock Exchange as XPER. Three deals between 2016 and 2022 put it there.

Rovi Corporation, a program-guide and technology licensing company, closed its purchase of TiVo on September 7, 2016, for $1.1 billion, made up of about $270 million in cash and 39.7 million new shares, and renamed itself TiVo. That company merged with Xperi on June 1, 2020, creating a portfolio of more than 11,000 patents and applications.

The 2022 split is why searches return two names. Adeia Inc., listed on Nasdaq as ADEA, kept the IP licensing business, while Xperi Inc. took the product business, which includes the TiVo brand, TiVo OS and the service behind every TiVo DVR. Questions about TiVo's patents now go to Adeia, questions about a TiVo box go to Xperi, and Xperi has plans for the name that have little to do with recording television.

What Xperi does with the TiVo name in 2026

Xperi sells TiVo as software for other companies' screens, the kind of second act the WebTV box never got. TiVo OS runs on televisions from several brands in Europe and, as of 2025, on Sharp sets in the United States, and on September 18, 2026, Element added 50-inch and 65-inch 4K TVs powered by TiVo. "The smart TV experience should revolve around content, not complexity," Geir Skaaden, Xperi's chief products and services officer, said at that launch.

The money comes from advertising on those screens. TiVo One, the ad platform built into them, reached 6.3 million monthly active users in the second quarter of 2026, and chief executive Jon Kirchner said the count "grew 70 percent from last year." Xperi's pay-TV business also counted 3.4 million IPTV subscriber households worldwide.

At the IFA trade show in Berlin on September 2, 2026, TiVo named Tal Bone, formerly of Hisense's VIDAA platform, to run TiVo OS, and said the system now fits on TVs with as little as 1 GB of memory. The company that asked $999 for 30 hours of recording in 1999 now pitches TV makers on how little hardware its software needs, while the boxes that made its name keep recording on a $14.99 plan.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does TiVo still support old DVRs in 2026?

TiVo still serves the Roamio, Bolt and Edge in November 2026 and will activate them on a $14.99 monthly plan. Series 2, Series 3 and Premiere DVRs keep their guide and recordings only if they already carry lifetime service, and once that plan is cancelled they cannot be reactivated. Series 1 boxes lost TiVo service on September 29, 2016.

Can you still buy a TiVo lifetime subscription?

Not for a newly activated box: in 2026 TiVo lists only a $14.99 monthly plan for the Roamio, Bolt and Edge, where lifetime cost $199 in 1999 and $549.99 for the Edge for cable in 2019. A used TiVo that already has lifetime service, sold as the All-In Plan, can move to a new owner if the original buyer authorizes the transfer online with the sales order number.

Is TiVo owned by Xperi or Adeia?

TiVo is owned by Xperi Inc., which took the TiVo brand, TiVo OS and the DVR service when Xperi Holding Corporation split on October 1, 2022. Adeia Inc., traded on Nasdaq as ADEA, is the other half of that split and kept the patent licensing business. Before that, Rovi bought TiVo for $1.1 billion in 2016 and merged with Xperi in 2020.
