Does Yahoo Answers Still Exist? It Closed in 2021

2026-09-10 | Updated 2026-09-29 | By Dana Reyes | 6 min read

Yahoo Answers no longer exists. Yahoo took the site read-only on April 20, 2021 and shut it down on May 4, 2021, more than 15 years after the December 2005 launch, and answers.yahoo.com no longer serves questions. The old threads survive only in web archives, and the Yahoo brand itself has belonged to Apollo Global Management since September 2021.

December 8, 2005, as a public beta from Yahoo

What Yahoo switched on in December 2005

Yahoo Answers opened to the public as a beta on December 8, 2005 at answers.yahoo.com. The Yahoo press release that day said the service would let people "tap into the collective intelligence of Web users" for the everyday questions a search box handled badly.

The design took one sentence to explain. Someone asked a question in plain English, the site filed it in a category, strangers answered, and after a few days the asker picked one reply as the Best Answer.

Points ran everything. Asking spent them, answering earned them, and a Best Answer earned more. Nobody could cash them in for anything, which is part of why people chased them so hard.

The timing was right. Search engines in 2005 were built for keywords rather than sentences, and Ask Jeeves had already shown that Americans wanted to type questions as questions. Yahoo Answers gave those questions somewhere to land where a person replied.

How big did the question board get at its peak?

Large, and faster than almost anyone predicted. Yahoo announced on July 10, 2012 that the site had collected 300 million questions since launch, a milestone Search Engine Land covered under the headline "Yahoo Answers Hits 300 Million Questions, But Q&A Activity Is Declining."

The rate figures describe what the site felt like from the inside. About 2 questions were asked and 6 answers posted every second in 2012, which works out to roughly 7,000 questions and 21,000 answers an hour.

Even that was the sound of a site past its best. In 2010, when Yahoo Answers passed its billionth question and answer, it was handling 823,966 of them a day, or about 34,000 an hour, so the 2012 rate was well below the one two years earlier.

Nothing else in English ran a general question board at that volume. Reddit had not yet become the default place to ask a stranger anything, and Stack Overflow only wanted programming questions. Yahoo Answers took the rest: homework, breakups, car noises, symptoms, religion, tax panic, and a great deal of nonsense.

When Stephen Hawking asked the internet a question

In July 2006 Yahoo ran a promotion called Ask The Planet, in which well-known people posted questions for the site to answer. One of them was Stephen Hawking.

His question read: "In a world that is in chaos politically, socially and environmentally, how can the human race sustain another 100 years?" NBC News reported that nearly 17,000 people had answered within days.

The replies were what a general audience produces when handed a cosmologist's question. NBC News quoted one that ran "get rid of nuclear weapons" and another that read "I don't think it is possible unless we expand into space."

That week is the high point of the whole idea. A physicist known worldwide put a serious question to the general public in public, and the general public answered, sincerely and clumsily and 17,000 times over.

Why the answers got worse than the questions

Yahoo Answers had no gate. Anyone with a Yahoo account could post, questions reached category front pages without review, and the reward for a careful answer and a joke answer was identical.

Trolls found that irresistible and spammers found those front pages valuable. Moderation ran on user reports routed through a corporate review process that could not keep pace with thousands of new posts an hour.

Screenshots of the worst spelling and the strangest questions turned into internet-wide jokes by the late 2000s. That brought people to the site as an audience rather than as askers, and a comedy site has no need of correct answers, so fewer people bothered to write them.

Google changed the other half of the equation. As search results got better at answering plain-language questions on the results page itself, the reason to open a question thread at all got thinner every year. Yahoo had bigger problems by then, and the company that later sold Tumblr for a fraction of what it paid was not going to spend its remaining engineering budget on the manners of a message board.

The 29 days from the notice to the shutdown

On April 5, 2021 a short notice went up on the Yahoo Answers home page. "While Yahoo Answers was once a key part of Yahoo's products and services, it has become less popular over the years as the needs of our members have changed," it said, and added that Yahoo was shifting resources to other products.

The schedule was brisk. Posting stopped on April 20, 2021, when the site went read-only. The site closed completely on May 4, 2021. Account holders had until June 30, 2021 to request a download of what they had posted, and Yahoo warned that compiling it could take another 30 days.

Search Engine Journal reported the announcement in April 2021 under the headline "Yahoo Answers Shutting Down May 4, Content Will Not Be Archived." Twenty-nine days separated the notice from the shutdown, which is not much runway for more than 15 years of accumulated content.

There was no sale, no spin-off, and no handoff of the archive to a library or a university. Yahoo took the content offline rather than parking it somewhere read-only, so volunteer archivists spent April 2021 racing the clock instead.

Who owns Yahoo Answers today, and can you still read it?

Nobody operates it. The address answers.yahoo.com no longer serves questions, and there is no replacement product from Yahoo and no announced plan for one.

The brand around it changed hands in the same year the site died. Apollo Global Management completed its roughly $5 billion purchase of what was then Verizon Media in September 2021, renamed the business Yahoo and holds about 90 percent of it, with Verizon keeping about 10 percent. That ownership has not changed since, though Yahoo has been talked about as an IPO candidate: as of February 2026 no filing had been made, and chief executive Jim Lanzone told The Information, "We really feel like we have a tiger by the tail here." The full ownership chain is in our piece on who owns Yahoo now.

What survives of the questions sits in the Internet Archive. Archive Team worked from the site's sitemap, which listed roughly 84 million questions, and saved what it could reach during the read-only window in April 2021. Coverage is uneven: heavily linked threads survived, obscure ones often did not, and pages have to be read one URL at a time through the Wayback Machine.

The questions themselves went elsewhere rather than stopping. Reddit took most of the ask-a-stranger traffic, Quora took the ones people wanted an expert to answer, Stack Exchange kept the technical ones, and search engines and AI assistants now handle the rest before another person ever sees them.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Yahoo Answers still exist in 2026?

No. Yahoo Answers stopped accepting posts on April 20, 2021 and shut down completely on May 4, 2021, and in 2026 the address answers.yahoo.com no longer serves questions. Yahoo, owned by Apollo Global Management since September 2021, has not launched a replacement question and answer service.

Why did Yahoo Answers shut down?

Yahoo closed Yahoo Answers on May 4, 2021 because use of the site had been falling for years. The notice Yahoo posted on April 5, 2021 said the service "has become less popular over the years as the needs of our members have changed," and the traffic figures backed it up: about 8 questions and answers a second in 2012, against roughly 823,966 a day in 2010.

Where can you read old Yahoo Answers questions in 2026?

Old Yahoo Answers pages survive mainly in the Internet Archive's Wayback Machine, where Archive Team volunteers and crawlers saved question threads in April 2021 before the May 4, 2021 shutdown. Yahoo removed the content from its own servers, published no archive of its own, and gave account holders only until June 30, 2021 to request a copy of their own questions and answers.
