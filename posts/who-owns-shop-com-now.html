Who Owns Shop.com Now? Shopify Paid $99 Million

By Dana Reyes · Published November 16, 2026 · 5 min read

Shopify owns Shop.com. Market America Worldwide, which had run the address since 2011, completed the sale on September 28, 2026, and Shopify's quarterly filing showed $99 million spent on the acquisition. The address is now the home of Shopify's Shop shopping service, which moved there from shop.app.

What Shopify bought, and what it did not

Market America Worldwide, the Greensboro, North Carolina direct selling company that had run SHOP.COM since 2011, announced on September 28, 2026 that its sale of the domain name to Shopify had closed. The announcement named no price.

What changed hands was the address. Domain Name Wire, reading Shopify's quarterly filing on November 3, 2026, noted that the purchase covered the web address only and not the former shop.com operating business. Market America kept its products, its UnFranchise sales program and its customer base, and moved all of it to MarketAmerica.com, which it relaunched on September 22, 2026 on Shopify's own platform.

Loren Ridinger, co-founder and chief executive of Market America Worldwide, said in the company's corrected release in October 2026 that "This was the perfect transaction for both companies." Harley Finkelstein, president of Shopify, was quoted in the same release calling SHOP.COM "a world-class digital asset that has a natural home at Shopify."

So the old shopping site is gone as a business, and the seller is still operating under a different name. Only the nine characters before the dot moved.

Market America's own description of the move was that it had consolidated its brands, products, technology and UnFranchise business at a reimagined MarketAmerica.com built on Shopify, and that it planned further spending on artificial intelligence and digital commerce technology. In other words, the seller of the address became a customer of the buyer.

How much did Shopify pay for the address?

Neither company gave a figure in September 2026. The number surfaced five weeks later, in Shopify's own numbers. Domain Name Wire reported on November 3, 2026 that the quarterly filing showed $99 million spent on the acquisition of intangible assets, and that the line was the shop.com domain.

That makes the price public without either side ever announcing it, which is how most large domain deals eventually become known. It also makes this the largest publicly known amount ever paid for a single domain name. The previous high was AI.com, which The Register reported in February 2026 had sold for $70 million.

For the kind of money Shopify spent on five letters and a dot, a buyer in 2026 could have bought a mid-sized software company. Shopify bought an address people already type into a browser bar by accident.

Shopify never issued a release of its own about the purchase. Its public account of the deal arrived secondhand, in the seller's announcement, and then as a line in a quarterly filing six weeks later. For a company that spends most of its public breath on merchant tools, nine figures for a name went by quietly.

How Market America ended up with Shop.com

The address has been expensive for more than twenty years. BetaKit reported in 2026 that the domain sold in 2003 for $3.5 million, going to Altura International from the Internet Real Estate Group. At the time that was a large sum for a domain and a small sum for the company that owned it.

Then came the acquisition that put it in North Carolina. In December 2010, TechCrunch reported that Market America had bought Shop.com, which it described as an online comparison shopping engine provider, for terms that were not disclosed. The same report listed the site's investors: Bill Gates, Amazon, Yahoo and Oak Investment Partners.

That investor list is the part worth sitting with. A shopping site backed by the founder of Microsoft, the largest online retailer in the country and the biggest portal of the dot-com years still ended up being sold for an undisclosed amount to a direct selling company most shoppers had never heard of. Plenty of dot-com era shopping ventures did worse. Pets.com lasted nine months as a public company, and Boo.com burned through its funding in eighteen.

Market America dates its ownership to 2011 and says it spent the following fifteen years building the domain into an international shopping destination. By 2026 it was running a Shopify store of its own, which is roughly when selling the address to Shopify started to make sense for everyone involved.

What is at Shop.com today?

Shopify's Shop service. Shopify's product changelog, dated September 29, 2026, told merchants that Shop's web experience had moved from shop.app to shop.com, that store, product and channel settings were unaffected, and that old shop.app links would redirect on their own.

Shop is Shopify's consumer-facing side: a shopping app and website where buyers browse products from the merchants who use Shopify, track orders and pay with Shop Pay. It had been sitting at shop.app, a newer and less familiar kind of address, since its launch.

If you type shop.com now expecting the old comparison shopping site, the coupon codes and the affiliate cashback program Market America ran there, none of that is on the page. The brand that greeted visitors for fifteen years moved to MarketAmerica.com in September 2026 and nothing redirects back to it.

Why an address is still worth nine figures in 2026

The usual argument against paying for a domain is that nobody types addresses anymore. Shopify paid $99 million anyway, about twenty-eight times what the same name went for in 2003.

The case for it is that a dot-com address does two jobs a clever name cannot. It is trusted on sight, and it is guessable. A shopper told to look at a Shopify storefront will type shop.com long before shop.app. Shopify's own description of the deal, through Finkelstein, treated the name as an asset in its own right rather than a redirect.

There is also the defensive logic. The name was in the hands of a seller who had just moved its business onto Shopify's platform, which is the narrow window where a sale gets agreed. Addresses like this come up for sale roughly never. The previous two owners held it for eight years and fifteen years.
